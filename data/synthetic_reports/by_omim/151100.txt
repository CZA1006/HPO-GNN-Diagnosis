A 14-year-old male presents with a history of multiple small dark skin spots that started appearing around early childhood, progressively increasing in number and densely covering the neck, face, and upper trunk. The parents note his slow growth compared to peers and mention episodes of chest pain and breathlessness during physical activity. He has facial features including widely spaced eyes (ocular hypertelorism), a broad nasal bridge, and a mildly depressed nasal root. Cardiac assessment reveals a harsh systolic murmur. Echocardiography identifies hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with significant left ventricular outflow tract obstruction and mild pulmonary valve stenosis.

Further examination reveals multiple café-au-lait spots and lentigines with irregular borders and varying shades of brown. Genital examination notes mild abnormalities such as cryptorchidism. Hearing tests show mild sensorineural hearing loss. The patient’s height and weight are below the 10th percentile, indicating growth delay. Electrocardiogram demonstrates conduction abnormalities including prolongation of the QT interval and episodes of non-sustained ventricular tachycardia. Cognitive assessment reveals mild intellectual disability with challenges in learning and attention.

This boy’s presentation is consistent with a multisystem syndrome characterized by cutaneous pigmentary anomalies, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, conduction defects, distinct craniofacial features, genital abnormalities, growth retardation, and sensorineural hearing loss. Genetic analysis has not been performed yet.
