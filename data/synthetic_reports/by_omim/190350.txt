A 12-year-old female is referred for evaluation of short stature and unusual facial and skeletal features. Her parents first became concerned around the age of 4 when her growth velocity fell below expected. Over time, they also noticed her hair remained fine, sparse, and slow-growing. She has not had significant health complications but has always appeared smaller and more slender than her peers. Teachers have noted normal cognitive performance and social development, though she struggles somewhat with coordination in physical activities.

She was born at term after an uncomplicated pregnancy and delivery, with a birth weight at the 25th percentile and length at the 10th percentile. Early development was within normal limits, with no gross motor or speech delays. Medical history is otherwise unremarkable. Family history reveals that her father has a short stature with features described as “unusual fingers” but was never investigated and is otherwise healthy; her mother and siblings are average in growth and development.

On physical examination, the child has proportionate short stature with height at the 5th percentile. Facial features are distinctive: a pear-shaped nose with a long philtrum, thin upper lip, prominent ears, and sparse, brittle hair along the scalp. Dental exam shows malalignment and delayed eruption of permanent teeth. Extremity exam reveals long, tapering fingers with mild clinodactyly of the fifth digits. Range of motion is normal, but slightly reduced flexibility is noted at the finger joints. No scoliosis or major spinal deformity is found. Skin, cardiovascular, respiratory, and neurologic examinations are unremarkable.

Radiographic studies demonstrate cone-shaped epiphyses of the middle phalanges of the hands, along with mild shortening of the metacarpals. Bone age is consistent with chronological age. Laboratory testing, including thyroid function and metabolic panel, is normal. Karyotype is reported as normal.

Given the constellation of short stature, craniofacial features, sparse hair, and phalangeal abnormalities, further genetic studies are being arranged to define the underlying cause.
