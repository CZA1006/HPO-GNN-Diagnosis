A 4-year-old male toddler presents with noticeable short stature and disproportionate limb length compared to his trunk, which became apparent around the age of two. His parents report that he has a stocky build with notably short arms and legs, especially in the upper segments. The child shows mild bowing of the legs and has broad, short hands and feet with shortened fingers. There are no apparent facial abnormalities apart from a relatively large head circumference with a slightly prominent forehead. Developmental milestones have been met for age, although mild motor delays are present.

Physical examination confirms rhizomelic shortening of the limbs, mild joint laxity, and increased lumbar lordosis. The spine displays mild scoliosis. Muscle tone and strength are normal. The child's neurological examination is unremarkable. There are no signs of hydrocephalus or craniosynostosis. Hearing and vision assessments are normal.

Laboratory studies reveal no metabolic abnormalities. Radiological imaging shows shortening and mild flaring of the long bone metaphyses, broadening and shortening of femoral necks, and reduced interpedicular distances in the lumbar spine. The iliac bones appear squared and shortened. These skeletal features are subtler compared to more severe forms of skeletal dysplasia.

The overall clinical presentation suggests a mild form of skeletal dysplasia characterized by disproportionate short stature, limb shortening, and characteristic radiographic findings. No genetic testing has been conducted yet.
