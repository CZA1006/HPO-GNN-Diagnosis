Leukocyte adhesion deficiency, type III (OMIM:612840)

The patient is a 5-year-old male presenting with recurrent severe infections, delayed wound healing, and a history of mild bleeding tendency. The patient has experienced multiple episodes of cellulitis and periodontitis since infancy, refractory to standard antibiotic therapy. Birth and early development were unremarkable. Family history is negative for immunodeficiency disorders. On examination, the patient appears chronically ill with poor wound granulation and mild mucosal bleeding. Laboratory investigations reveal normal leukocyte counts but markedly impaired platelet aggregation and defective integrin activation on flow cytometry. Coagulation tests are within normal limits. Platelet function studies confirm defective inside-out signaling of integrins. Imaging and cultures identify recurrent soft tissue infections without abscess formation. The clinical picture is consistent with a combined defect of platelet and leukocyte adhesion. Initial management includes prophylactic antibiotics and supportive care.
