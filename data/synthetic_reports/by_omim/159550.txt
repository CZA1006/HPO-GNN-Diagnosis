A 10-year-old male presents with gradually worsening problems with balance, coordination, and walking that have developed over several years. His parents report frequent falls, difficulty with fine motor tasks, and occasional involuntary eye movements. Neurological examination reveals cerebellar ataxia characterized by unsteady gait, dysmetria on finger-to-nose testing, nystagmus, hyperreflexia, and ankle clonus. Cognitive development shows mild attention deficits but no significant intellectual disability.

Hematologic evaluations indicate pancytopenia with reduced red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets, leading to fatigue, recurrent infections, and easy bruising. Bone marrow biopsy shows hypocellularity with dysplastic features. Imaging studies reveal marked cerebellar atrophy and periventricular white matter changes on brain MRI. The patient has experienced episodes of recurrent respiratory infections and shows evidence of immunodeficiency with low immunoglobulin levels.

Family history is significant for similar neurological and hematological symptoms in a paternal relative. Regular monitoring is in place due to a predisposition to bone marrow failure and myeloid malignancies. Genetic testing is awaited.

The clinical presentation indicates a progressive neurohematologic disorder characterized by cerebellar dysfunction, blood cell deficiencies, and a high risk of hematologic malignancies.
