A 2-year-old male presents with generalized muscle stiffness and difficulty relaxing his muscles after voluntary contraction, first noted in infancy. Parents report delayed relaxation of the hands after gripping objects, difficulty opening the eyes fully after forced closure such as crying or sneezing, and mild stiffness in the legs, causing clumsiness and frequent falls. These symptoms are typically worse after periods of rest, improving with repeated movement, described as a "warm-up" phenomenon. There is no significant muscle weakness but mild muscle hypertrophy is observed, giving an athletic appearance.

Physical exam reveals delayed muscle relaxation predominantly in the facial, hand, and lower limb muscles. Muscle tone is normal, and no reflex abnormalities are noted. The child exhibits no craniofacial dysmorphism or other systemic abnormalities. There is no history of respiratory difficulty or cardiac involvement. Symptoms show little progression, and the child remains ambulatory and independent in daily activities.

Electromyography demonstrates myotonic discharges consistent with myotonia congenita. Laboratory tests including serum creatine kinase levels are normal. Genetic family history suggests autosomal dominant inheritance. No current genetic testing has been performed.

This clinical picture indicates a nonprogressive muscle channelopathy characterized by delayed muscle relaxation (myotonia), muscle stiffness, and hypertrophy with autosomal dominant inheritance.
