A 12-year-old female is evaluated for progressive visual impairment and alopecia. Parents note that since early childhood she has had thinning hair and difficulties with night vision worsening over time. She also experiences short stature and mild intellectual disability.

On examination, the patient exhibits alopecia totalis and retinal pigmentary changes consistent with retinitis pigmentosa. Facial features show midface hypoplasia. Neurological assessment indicates mild cognitive impairment. Endocrine evaluation reveals delayed puberty. Magnetic resonance imaging of the brain is normal. Laboratory tests, including metabolic panels, are unremarkable.
