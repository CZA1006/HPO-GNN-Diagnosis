A 3-week-old female neonate is admitted for respiratory distress and feeding difficulties. She was born at term via normal vaginal delivery. Parents report the infant has had persistent stridor and poor weight gain. Physical exam reveals a small thyroid gland and mild facial dysmorphism, including a broad nasal bridge and micrognathia.

Laryngoscopy identifies bilateral vocal cord paresis with airway narrowing. Thyroid function tests reveal elevated TSH and low free T4 consistent with congenital hypothyroidism. Chest radiograph and echocardiography are unremarkable. Serum calcium and phosphorus levels are normal. The patient is started on thyroid hormone replacement therapy with gradual clinical improvement.
