A 6-year-old female child presents with short stature and a distinctive round face. She is the first child of non-consanguineous parents, with no known family history of similar features or endocrine disorders. Her early development was unremarkable, but parents began noticing difficulties with growth and motor skills in early childhood. The child has experienced episodes of muscle cramps and spasms, sometimes accompanied by tingling sensations around the mouth and in the limbs. There are no histories of seizures or major illnesses.

On physical examination, the child has a stocky build with a body mass index in the upper centiles for age. The face is round with a prominent jaw and a stocky appearance. The hands exhibit shortening of the fourth and fifth metacarpals and phalanges (brachydactyly). Subcutaneous ossifications are observed on the forearms. Neurological examination is normal with no focal deficits. Reflexes are symmetric and muscle tone is normal. Growth parameters confirm height below the third percentile for age.

Laboratory investigations reveal hypocalcemia and elevated phosphate levels in serum. Parathyroid hormone (PTH) levels are elevated, indicating resistance to the hormone's effect. Thyroid function tests suggest mild hypothyroidism. Radiographic imaging shows advanced bone age, shortening of tubular bones, brachydactyly, and subcutaneous ossifications. No genetic tests have been performed yet. The clinical picture is consistent with multiple hormone resistances with characteristic skeletal and metabolic features, awaiting further diagnostic confirmation.
