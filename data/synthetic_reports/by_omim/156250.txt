A 7-year-old male presents with multiple nodular bony swellings on the fingers and toes, first noted around age 5. The parents report occasional pain in the affected areas, especially after physical activity. The child has normal stature and no significant limb deformities or joint deformities. Examination reveals multiple small, hard, painless osteochondroma-like bumps on the metaphyses of the short tubular bones of the hands and feet, pointing towards adjacent joints. Radiographs confirm these osteochondromas primarily on the hands, feet, and also show multiple enchondromas affecting the metaphyses of long bones and the iliac crests.

The lesions tend to spontaneously regress over time. There is no evidence of shortening, bowing, or subluxation of bones. The child has normal motor development and no neurological deficits. No malignant transformation of any lesion has been observed. Family history reveals a similar pattern of bony lesions in several relatives suggesting an inherited condition.

The clinical presentation is consistent with a rare hereditary disorder characterized by a combination of multiple enchondromas and osteochondroma-like lesions affecting predominantly the small bones of the hands and feet with a tendency for lesion spontaneous regression. Follow-up and symptomatic management are planned, with genetic testing pending.
