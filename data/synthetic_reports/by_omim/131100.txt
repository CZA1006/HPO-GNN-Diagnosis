A 39-year-old male presents with a history of persistent fatigue, bone pain, and recurrent kidney stones. He reports episodic abdominal pain and frequent diarrhea over the past several months. His family history is significant for multiple relatives affected by endocrine tumors and similar symptoms. The patient underwent pituitary surgery for a macroadenoma that caused headaches and visual disturbances. Physical examination reveals coarse facial features consistent with acromegaly, telangiectatic skin lesions resembling nevi, and mild obesity.

Laboratory tests show elevated prolactin and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) levels, along with deficiencies in several pituitary hormones. Imaging studies identify multiple endocrine tumors including parathyroid adenomas, pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors, and a recurrent pituitary macroadenoma. The pancreatic lesions display varying degrees of hormone production, correlating with the patient’s gastrointestinal symptoms, including elevated gastrin levels causing peptic ulcers. Further evaluation reveals adrenal adenomas without evidence of hormone excess.

A comprehensive endocrine evaluation confirms the presence of hyperparathyroidism, with associated hypercalcemia and hypophosphatemia contributing to the patient’s bone pain and kidney stones. Electrolyte imbalances are noted during episodes of hormone overproduction. The clinical picture suggests a multisystem disorder involving tumors across several endocrine glands, manifesting with hormonal imbalances, systemic symptoms, and requiring ongoing surveillance and multidisciplinary management. Genetic testing has not yet been performed.
