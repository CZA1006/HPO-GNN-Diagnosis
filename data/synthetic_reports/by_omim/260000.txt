A 4-year-old male is brought to the nephrology clinic for evaluation of recurrent kidney stones and nephrocalcinosis. Parents report episodes of flank pain and hematuria starting at age 2. His medical history is notable for failure to thrive and intermittent abdominal discomfort. There is no known family history of renal disease.

On physical examination, growth parameters are at the 10th percentile. Abdominal palpation is unremarkable. Laboratory studies reveal elevated urinary oxalate excretion and mild renal impairment with increased serum creatinine. Ultrasound shows bilateral medullary nephrocalcinosis and small renal calculi. Plasma glycolate levels are elevated, while liver function tests are normal.
