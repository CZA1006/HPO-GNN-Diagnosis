A 4-year-old male presents with hypotonia and delayed motor milestones since infancy. The parents report difficulty in sitting unsupported and delayed walking compared to peers. Progressive muscle weakness predominantly affects proximal muscles of the limbs, with associated mild facial weakness including ptosis. The child exhibits fatigability and occasional muscle cramps.

On examination, muscle tone is reduced with decreased muscle bulk (atrophy) mainly proximally. Deep tendon reflexes are diminished but present. There is mild scoliosis, and spine mobility is slightly restricted. Eye movement abnormalities including mild ophthalmoparesis and bilateral ptosis are observed. No respiratory distress is noted currently, but pulmonary function is being closely monitored. Cognitive development is normal for age.

Electromyography shows myopathic changes, and muscle biopsy reveals characteristic findings of centrally located nuclei within muscle fibers and predominance of type 1 muscle fiber hypotrophy. Radiologic imaging is unremarkable aside from mild skeletal abnormalities frequently associated with the disorder. Serum creatine kinase levels are normal or mildly elevated.

This presentation is consistent with a congenital myopathy characterized by muscle weakness, hypotonia, facial involvement, and typical histopathologic features, with a variable severity spectrum. Genetic testing for causative gene mutations is awaited.
