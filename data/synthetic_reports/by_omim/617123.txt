Retinitis pigmentosa 76 (OMIM:617123)

The patient is a 28-year-old male who presented with progressive visual decline predominantly characterized by night blindness beginning in adolescence. There is a positive family history of similar visual symptoms affecting his grandfather and maternal aunt. Ophthalmic examination reveals typical retinal changes including bone-spicule pigment deposits in the mid-periphery, attenuation of retinal vessels, and optic disc pallor. Visual field testing confirms concentric constriction with relative sparing of central vision. Fundus autofluorescence shows gradual loss of photoreceptors. Optical coherence tomography quantifies thinning primarily of the outer retinal layers. Electroretinogram studies demonstrate significant reduction in both rod and cone responses. The patient reports difficulty adapting to dim lighting and moderate impairment in color discrimination with no systemic involvement noted. Counseling and low vision support have been implemented.
