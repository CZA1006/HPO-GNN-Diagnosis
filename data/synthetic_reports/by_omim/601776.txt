Deafness, autosomal recessive 15 (OMIM:601869)

The patient is a 3-year-old male brought in by his parents due to delayed speech development and lack of response to auditory stimuli. Pregnancy and delivery were uncomplicated. The family history is significant for a paternal cousin with congenital hearing loss. On examination, the child is otherwise healthy, with normal growth parameters and no dysmorphic features. Otoscopic evaluation reveals normal external and middle ear structures. Brainstem auditory evoked responses show absent bilateral waveforms at maximum stimulation, suggestive of profound sensorineural hearing loss. Tympanometry is normal, excluding middle ear pathology. Developmental testing reveals delays in expressive language but otherwise age-appropriate motor and cognitive skills. Routine metabolic and infectious workup was unremarkable.
Thrombocythemia 2 (OMIM:601977)

The patient is a 45-year-old female referred for hematology assessment after persistent elevation of platelet counts found on routine blood work. She reports frequent headaches and episodic tingling in her fingertips but denies bleeding or bruising tendencies. Family history is notable for her mother, who was treated for a similar blood disorder in her 50s. On physical examination, the patient is well-appearing with no organomegaly or lymphadenopathy. Complete blood count shows persistent thrombocytosis with platelet counts >800 × 10^9/L, while leukocyte and hemoglobin levels are within normal limits. Peripheral smear demonstrates large, mature platelets. Bone marrow biopsy reveals increased numbers of megakaryocytes without dysplastic features. Coagulation studies are normal. No secondary causes of thrombocytosis (such as infection or iron deficiency) were identified.
Pierpont syndrome (OMIM:602342)

Standard metabolic panels, thyroid studies, and chromosomal microarray were normal.
Desmosterolosis (OMIM:602398)

The patient is a 4-month-old female evaluated for hypotonia and developmental concerns. She was born after an uneventful pregnancy but noted to have microcephaly and poor feeding after birth. Growth is below the 5th percentile for age. Family history is notable for parental consanguinity but no known diagnoses. On examination, she exhibits global hypotonia, delayed visual tracking, and unusual craniofacial features including a broad nasal bridge, micrognathia, and high-arched palate. There is hepatosplenomegaly on abdominal palpation. Neurological exam shows poor head control and exaggerated startle reflexes. Brain MRI demonstrates lissencephaly-like cortical malformations and dysgenesis of the corpus callosum. Laboratory investigations reveal markedly elevated plasma sterol intermediates, consistent with a defect in cholesterol biosynthesis.
