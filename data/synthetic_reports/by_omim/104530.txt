A 10-year-old male child presents with concerns about the appearance and sensitivity of his teeth. He was born to non-consanguineous parents with no family history of dental abnormalities. His early development was normal, but the parents noticed that his baby teeth erupted with an unusual yellowish discoloration and were prone to chips and wear. The permanent teeth are similarly affected, with visible pitting on the surfaces, primarily on the labial and buccal sides arranged in rows and columns. The enamel appears thin and fragile, leading to increased sensitivity to temperature changes and difficulty eating certain foods. There are no other systemic health problems reported.

On examination, the teeth exhibit hypoplastic enamel with surface pits that form a patterned appearance. The enamel layer is thinner than normal and prone to premature breakdown. No signs of caries are evident at present, but the teeth are sensitive to touch and temperature stimuli. Oral mucosa is healthy, and no other craniofacial abnormalities are noted. Growth and neurological examinations are normal.

Laboratory tests including metabolic and mineral panels are normal. Radiographs reveal thin enamel layers with normal dentin and root structures, confirming enamel hypoplasia without other dental anomalies. Genetic testing has not yet been conducted. The clinical presentation suggests a form of inherited enamel defect affecting enamel thickness and mineralization.
