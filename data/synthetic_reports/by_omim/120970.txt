A 9-year-old female presents with progressive visual difficulties that began in early childhood. Initially, she experienced decreased sharpness of vision, notably for fine details required for reading and recognizing faces, along with increased sensitivity to bright light (photophobia). Color vision abnormalities were reported, with difficulty distinguishing certain colors. Over time, this progressed to include blurred central vision and development of blind spots predominantly in the central visual field. Recently, the patient began experiencing night blindness and a gradual loss of peripheral vision, leading to challenges with mobility in dim lighting and navigating familiar environments.

Ophthalmologic examination reveals reduced visual acuity and a central scotoma. Fundoscopic examination shows macular pigmentary changes with atrophy and a bull's-eye maculopathy appearance. Peripheral retina displays pigment epithelial atrophy and arteriolar attenuation. Electroretinography demonstrates significantly reduced cone photoreceptor responses with slower or diminished rod activity, consistent with cone-rod dystrophy.

Family history is negative for similar visual impairment. No hearing loss or systemic involvement is noted. Growth and neurological exams are normal. Laboratory studies are within normal limits. Genetic testing has not yet been performed.
