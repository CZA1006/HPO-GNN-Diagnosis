A 5-year-old female presents with a history of progressive nearsightedness noticed in early childhood. She was born to unrelated healthy parents with no significant family history of eye or connective tissue disorders. The child has experienced frequent ear infections and mild hearing loss. Parents have also noted a moderately flat facial profile with underdeveloped midface bones and a small chin. Feeding difficulties and mild speech delay were reported in infancy.

On examination, the child exhibits characteristic craniofacial features with midfacial hypoplasia, micrognathia, and a slightly protruding tongue. Ocular examination confirms high myopia with early signs of vitreoretinal abnormalities, including patches of retinal pigment epithelium atrophy and mild lens opacities. Hearing assessment shows a mild to moderate sensorineural hearing loss. The musculoskeletal exam reveals joint hypermobility and mild scoliosis. No cardiac abnormalities are detected.

Laboratory tests are unremarkable. Imaging studies document early degenerative changes in the hip and knee joints consistent with osteoarthritis. Genetic testing has not been performed yet. The clinical presentation is compatible with a hereditary connective tissue disorder involving ocular, auditory, craniofacial, and musculoskeletal systems, pending definitive genetic diagnosis.
