A 6-year-old female is brought to the clinic by her parents due to concerns about developmental delays and behavioral difficulties. Her parents report that she had delayed motor milestones, sitting independently at 12 months and walking at around 2 years of age. Speech development has also been slow, with only short sentences used at present, accompanied by frequent articulation errors. Teachers have noted challenges with attention and learning at school, as well as oppositional behaviors and frequent outbursts. The parents emphasize an unusual sleep pattern: she often wakes up at night and becomes alert and active, with significant daytime sleepiness.

Pregnancy and birth history are unremarkable. The patient was delivered at term, with normal birth weight and without perinatal complications. There is no history of significant neonatal illness aside from feeding difficulties during infancy, when she required prolonged bottle feeding due to poor suck and later exhibited persistent failure to thrive during the first years of life. Medical history is otherwise notable for recurrent otitis media and a single episode of pneumonia. She has not required major hospitalizations or surgeries. The family history reveals no known neurodevelopmental disorders or genetic conditions.

On physical examination, the patient is small for age, with height and weight both below the 10th percentile. Craniofacial features include a broad, square-shaped face, midface retrusion, and slightly deep-set eyes. She has brachydactyly, with short, broad hands and clinodactyly of the fifth fingers. Muscle tone is mildly decreased in the upper and lower extremities, and fine motor coordination is delayed. Neurologically, cranial nerves are intact, gait is stable but clumsy, and reflexes are normal. Behavioral examination during the visit reveals frequent attempts to hug the examiner, intermittent self-injurious behaviors such as hitting her head with her hand, episodes of frustration with changes in routine, and fluctuating mood.

Laboratory testing, including serum electrolytes, thyroid function, and metabolic screening, is within normal limits. Polysomnography confirms disrupted circadian rhythm with frequent nighttime wakening and excessive daytime sleepiness. Audiological evaluation reveals mild conductive hearing loss, likely related to recurrent middle ear infections. Ophthalmologic assessment identifies mild hyperopia but otherwise normal fundus and ocular movements. A brain MRI shows no structural abnormalities. Standard karyotype analysis is reported as normal, and further chromosomal microarray analysis is planned as the next step in the diagnostic process.

Given the distinctive developmental, behavioral, and physical features, along with the sleep disturbance and clinical findings, the case warrants genetic evaluation for a more definitive diagnosis.
