A 15-year-old male presents with progressive pain in his arms and legs that has been ongoing for several years, worsening with physical activity and exposure to cold weather. The parents report that he has a wide-based, waddling gait and often feels fatigued, especially in his proximal muscles. The boy has experienced difficulty standing up from a seated position due to muscle weakness. There is no significant family history of bone disorders, but some distant relatives reportedly had skeletal problems. Physical examination reveals a prominent forehead, a large lower jaw, and bulging eyes. The limbs are slender but with visibly thickened and curved bones. There is evidence of joint contractures, knock knees, and flat feet. The spine shows signs of mild scoliosis.

Neurological examination is notable for mild facial weakness, occasional dizziness, and reduced hearing in both ears. Ophthalmological evaluation shows mild proptosis and papilledema. Laboratory tests reveal normal calcium, phosphate, and alkaline phosphatase levels, but mild anemia is present. Radiographic imaging demonstrates symmetric fusiform thickening and increased density of the diaphyses of the long bones with sparing of the epiphyses. The skull shows diffuse sclerosis with narrowing of cranial foramina and thickened calvarium. Bone scans reveal increased osteoblastic activity in affected regions.

The overall clinical picture suggests a rare progressive bone disorder characterized by hyperostosis of long bones and skull, leading to pain, muscle weakness, neurological symptoms, and distinctive facial features. Genetic testing has not yet been conducted.
