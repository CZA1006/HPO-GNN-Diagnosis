A 3-year-old male child presents with feeding difficulties, failure to thrive, and developmental delay. He was born at term to unrelated parents after a pregnancy complicated by polyhydramnios. At birth, the patient showed distinctive facial features including a high forehead, a long and broad face, a short nose with a depressed nasal bridge, downward slanting of the eyes, ptosis, and low-set ears. His hair is sparse, thin, and curly. Cardiovascular evaluation reveals pulmonic valve stenosis and a small atrial septal defect. Dermatological examination shows dry, rough skin with patches of hyperkeratosis and the presence of keratosis pilaris on the arms and legs. The child has hypotonia and delayed motor milestones, with speech delay also evident. Seizures have not yet occurred but are a known risk.

Neurological examination notes mild hypotonia and a slightly increased head circumference. Auditory testing shows mild conductive hearing loss. Skeletal examination is significant for mild scoliosis and flat feet. Routine laboratory investigations are within normal limits. Brain MRI displays mild ventriculomegaly without other structural abnormalities. Genetic testing targeting mutations in the BRAF and related genes has not been performed to date.

This clinical picture is consistent with a rare autosomal dominant multiple congenital anomalies syndrome characterized by distinctive craniofacial features, heart defects, dermatological abnormalities, neurological involvement, and growth delay, pending molecular confirmation.
