A 5-year-old female is evaluated for developmental delay and vision problems. Parents report delayed motor milestones, with independent walking achieved at 3 years, and ongoing problems with clumsiness and unsteady gait. There are also concerns about learning difficulties and delayed speech development. Ophthalmology referral was made after noticing her eyes do not fully respond to light.

On examination, the child shows truncal ataxia, wide-based gait, and poor coordination during reaching. Speech is dysarthric. Cranial nerve function is largely intact, but eye examination reveals bilateral partial aniridia with reduced pupillary reflexes. Ophthalmoscopy shows foveal hypoplasia and reduced visual acuity. Neurologic examination confirms cerebellar signs including intention tremor and impaired rapid alternating movements. Cognitive testing demonstrates mild intellectual disability. MRI of the brain shows hypoplasia of the cerebellar vermis.
