A 3-week-old male neonate is admitted for progressive encephalopathy and feeding intolerance. Parents report poor feeding, hypotonia, and recurrent seizures. There is no known family history of neurological disorders.

On examination, he exhibits profound hypotonia, poor spontaneous movements, and exaggerated startle reflex. Laboratory findings include elevated urinary sulfite and thiosulfate levels. Brain MRI shows cerebral atrophy and cystic lesions in the white matter. Electroencephalogram reveals multifocal epileptiform activity.
