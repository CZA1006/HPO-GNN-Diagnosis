A 6-month-old male infant presents with congenital cataracts noted at birth and persistent hypotonia observed since the neonatal period. The patient has exhibited delayed motor milestones and poor feeding, requiring nasogastric tube support. There is a history of frequent vomiting and episodes of dehydration. Family history is non-contributory, with no known relatives affected by similar symptoms.

On physical examination, the infant has bilateral dense cataracts with irregular pupil reactivity and mild proptosis. Muscle tone is significantly reduced, with generalized hypotonia and diminished deep tendon reflexes. There is evidence of areflexia. The infant’s growth parameters are below the 5th percentile for age. Neurological assessment reveals poor head control and delayed developmental progress. Skeletal examination is unremarkable at this time.

Laboratory investigations demonstrate metabolic acidosis with electrolyte imbalances, including hypokalemia and hypophosphatemia. Urinalysis reveals aminoaciduria and proteinuria consistent with proximal renal tubular dysfunction. Renal function tests indicate impaired bicarbonate reabsorption. Brain MRI shows mild periventricular leukomalacia. Ophthalmologic assessment confirms congenital cataracts without signs of glaucoma at this time.

This presentation is indicative of a multisystem disorder involving the eyes, central nervous system, and kidneys, with early-onset ocular abnormalities, muscle weakness, and renal tubular dysfunction manifesting as metabolic derangements and growth retardation. Genetic analysis has not been performed yet.
