A 3-year-old female presents with thickened, yellowish skin on the palms and soles, which has been progressively worsening since infancy. The parents report that the skin is rough, cracked, and painful, especially in areas subject to friction and pressure. Episodes of skin blistering and erythema were noted in early childhood, followed by gradual development of durable hyperkeratosis restricted mainly to the palmoplantar surfaces. There is significant discomfort when walking or using the hands for fine manipulation. The child also experiences excessive sweating on the hands and feet, accompanied by a mild unpleasant odor. No other systemic symptoms are reported.

Physical examination reveals well-demarcated, diffuse palmoplantar hyperkeratosis with a waxy, yellowish hue. The hyperkeratosis spares the dorsal aspects of hands and feet but extends slightly onto the lateral edges. Several fissures and painful cracks are present. Dermatoglyphics are preserved but less distinct due to thickened skin. No nail abnormalities or other skin lesions elsewhere on the body are noted. There is no evidence of inflammation or secondary infection at present.

Histological examination of a skin biopsy shows epidermolytic changes characterized by vacuolar degeneration of the upper spinous and granular layers with clumped keratohyalin granules. The stratum corneum is markedly thickened. Routine laboratory studies are within normal limits. Treatment so far has been symptomatic with emollients and keratolytic agents, providing partial relief.

The clinical features are consistent with a hereditary disorder of keratinization manifesting as diffuse, epidermolytic palmoplantar keratoderma presenting in early childhood, with characteristic histopathological findings. Genetic testing has not yet been performed.
