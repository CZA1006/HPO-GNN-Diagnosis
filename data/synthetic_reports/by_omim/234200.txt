A 12-year-old female is evaluated for progressive motor decline and dystonia beginning in mid-childhood. Parents describe onset of difficulty with walking, hand tremors, and speech deterioration over the past three years. Cognitive abilities have remained relatively preserved, but recent behavioral changes have been noted. Family history is unremarkable.

Neurological examination reveals generalized dystonia, rigidity, and dysarthria. Reflexes are brisk with ankle clonus, and gait is spastic and unsteady. MRI of the brain shows prominent hypointensity in the basal ganglia regions on T2-weighted images, suggestive of abnormal iron deposition. Serum ferritin and transferrin saturation are within normal limits. Routine metabolic panels are unremarkable.
