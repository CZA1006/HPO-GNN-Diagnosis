A 10-month-old female infant presents with a range of developmental delays and craniofacial abnormalities noted since birth. She was born at term after an uncomplicated pregnancy and delivery, but parents have observed poor feeding, hypotonia, and failure to meet expected developmental milestones. Facial features include a depressed nasal bridge, closely spaced eyes (hypotelorism), and a single central upper front tooth. The infant has a cleft palate and a broad, flattened nasal tip. There are subtle midline defects including a median cleft lip.

Neurologic examination reveals global developmental delay with poor head control and hypotonia. Seizures have been intermittent since 6 months of age. Endocrine abnormalities have been suspected, with episodes of low blood sugar and sodium imbalances. Brain MRI demonstrates incomplete separation of the cerebral hemispheres consistent with lobar to semilobar brain malformations. No ventriculomegaly is present. Cardiac and abdominal examinations are unremarkable, and no obvious visceral anomalies have been detected so far.

Laboratory investigations reveal no major metabolic derangements. Genetic testing has not yet been conducted. This case represents a congenital brain malformation due to incomplete division of the forebrain, associated with characteristic facial, neurologic, and endocrine abnormalities, requiring multidisciplinary management and further diagnostic workup.
