A 4-year-old female child presents with a history of congenital skin defects on the sides of her neck noticed shortly after birth. The lesions appear as thin, erythematous patches that sometimes weep and are associated with hair loss. She has a history of ocular problems including significant visual impairment and strabismus. Parents describe her facial features as atypical, with widely spaced eyes, an unusually broad nasal tip, and a cleft lip that was surgically repaired in infancy. She has small malformed ears, recurrent ear infections, and mild hearing loss. No family history of similar defects is known.

On physical examination, the child has thin erythematous linear skin defects along both sides of the neck. The eyes show ptosis and microphthalmia, with nasolacrimal duct obstruction. The facial examination reveals hypertelorism, a broad nasal bridge with a flattened tip, upslanted palpebral fissures, and prominent philtral ridges. The ears are low-set and malformed with discreet preauricular pits. Oral examination confirms repaired cleft lip with a prominent philtral ridge. There is mild asymmetry of facial muscles due to partial seventh cranial nerve weakness. No cardiac or renal abnormalities are detected on initial screening.

Laboratory and imaging studies include renal ultrasound and cardiac evaluation, both of which are unremarkable. Audiologic testing confirms a mixed conductive and sensorineural hearing loss. Developmental milestones are mostly normal, though mild speech delay is noted. Genetic testing for mutations in the TFAP2A gene, which encodes a transcription factor involved in embryogenesis, has not been performed yet.

The clinical presentation is consistent with a rare autosomal dominant syndrome characterized by branchial skin defects, ocular anomalies, facial dysmorphisms, hearing loss, and varying degrees of developmental and structural abnormalities, pending confirmatory genetic diagnosis.
