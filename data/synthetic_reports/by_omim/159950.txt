A 5-year-old male child presents with a history of normal early development followed by progressive muscle weakness and wasting primarily affecting the lower limbs. Parents report difficulty walking, frequent falls, and muscle cramps developing over the last two years. The weakness gradually progressed to involve the upper limbs, leading to difficulty with fine motor tasks such as writing and buttoning clothes. Neurological examination shows marked distal muscle atrophy, decreased deep tendon reflexes, and weakness following a pattern suggestive of anterior horn cell involvement.

Approximately two years after the onset of muscle weakness, the child began experiencing recurrent seizures characterized by sudden, involuntary muscle jerks (myoclonic epilepsy). These seizures have increased in frequency and severity despite medication and are often accompanied by generalized tonic-clonic seizures and absence seizures. Interictal EEG shows generalized polyspike and wave discharges. The child also exhibits tremors of the hands and mild sensorineural hearing loss. Cognitive development is normal initially but shows subtle declines with disease progression.

Motor nerve conduction studies reveal reduced amplitudes characteristic of motor neuron disease. Muscle biopsy shows neurogenic atrophy with grouped fiber atrophy and angular fibers. Brain and spinal MRI are unremarkable. Laboratory investigations rule out metabolic and mitochondrial diseases. Genetic testing is pending but clinical findings suggest a rare form of spinal muscular atrophy combined with progressive myoclonic epilepsy.

The disease course is marked by steadily progressive muscle atrophy and weakness with superimposed drug-resistant epilepsy, leading to significant disability over several years. Management focuses on symptom control and supportive care.
