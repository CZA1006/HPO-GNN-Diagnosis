A 10-year-old female is referred for evaluation of recurrent fevers, rash, and joint pain. Her parents report that since around age 6, she has experienced frequent episodes of fever lasting one to three days, often associated with generalized malaise and irritability. These episodes are accompanied by a non-pruritic urticarial rash over the arms and trunk, along with arthralgia involving the knees and ankles. Symptoms tend to occur spontaneously without identifiable triggers, though cold weather sometimes exacerbates them. Parents also report progressive hearing difficulties over the past year, and she now struggles to follow speech in noisy environments.

Pregnancy and birth history are unremarkable, and early development was normal. The child has not had significant infections apart from typical childhood illnesses. There is no history of autoimmune disease or inflammatory bowel disease in the family, but her mother recalls experiencing episodic rash and tinnitus as a teenager that gradually subsided.

On physical examination, the child is afebrile during the visit but appears tired. A faint, erythematous, urticaria-like rash is visible across the forearms and back. No nodules, vesicles, or ulcers are noted. Joint exam reveals mild swelling and tenderness of the right knee with restricted flexion; other joints are without deformity. Hearing assessment during the visit suggests decreased acuity to whispered voice bilaterally. Ophthalmologic screening is normal. Cardiovascular, respiratory, and abdominal examinations are unremarkable, with no hepatosplenomegaly or lymphadenopathy. Neurologic examination is normal aside from reported hearing loss.

Laboratory testing during febrile episodes shows elevated inflammatory markers, with ESR and CRP markedly increased. White blood cell count is at the upper limit of normal with neutrophil predominance. Between attacks, lab values normalize partially but CRP often remains mildly elevated. Renal function is currently normal, and urinalysis shows no proteinuria. Audiometry confirms bilateral sensorineural hearing loss, more pronounced at higher frequencies. Ophthalmological evaluation reveals no evidence of conjunctivitis or uveitis.

Given the recurrent fevers, urticarial rash, joint pain, systemic inflammation, and emerging hearing impairment, further genetic testing is being arranged to clarify the underlying cause and guide management.
