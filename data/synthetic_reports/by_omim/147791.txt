A 4-year-old female presents with delayed developmental milestones, particularly in speech and motor skills. Her parents report difficulty with feeding and frequent episodes of bruising and bleeding since infancy. There is a history of recurrent respiratory infections and occasional gastrointestinal problems including constipation. The child exhibits distinctive facial features including a high prominent forehead, wide-set eyes with drooping eyelids, downward slanting palpebral fissures, a broad and flat nasal bridge, a short nose, a long and flat philtrum with a thin upper lip, and small, low-set ears that are rotated posteriorly. Her mouth has a characteristic V-shape and she demonstrates retrognathia. Her neck appears short, and the hands show cutaneous webbing between fingers, thin fingers with flat pads, and abnormal palmar creases. The feet are broad, stubby, and flat with clinodactyly and syndactyly between the second and third toes.

Physical examination reveals short stature and mild hypotonia. There is mild facial asymmetry and mild scoliosis. Cardiovascular evaluation identifies congenital heart defects including ventricular septal defects and hypoplastic left heart structures. The child has persistent thrombocytopenia with a history of easy bruising and prolonged bleeding times, consistent with platelet function abnormalities. Renal, gastrointestinal, and genital anomalies have been identified, including mild pelvic kidney hypoplasia and ambiguous genitalia. Neurological examination notes mild cognitive impairment and behavioral issues with attention difficulties and occasional compulsive behaviors.

Laboratory investigations confirm thrombocytopenia and platelet dysfunction. Imaging studies include brain MRI revealing mild cerebral structural anomalies and skeletal X-rays showing no major bone malformations. Hearing evaluation is normal. The overall clinical manifestations involve multisystem developmental delays, characteristic craniofacial dysmorphisms, hematologic abnormalities, and congenital organ malformations. Genetic testing has not yet been performed.
