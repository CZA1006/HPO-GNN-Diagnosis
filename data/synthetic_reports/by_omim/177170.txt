A 3-year-old male child is evaluated for short stature and delayed motor milestones. He was born after an uneventful pregnancy with normal birth measurements but was noted to have mild joint laxity and a waddling gait starting at age two. Over time, the child’s height and limb length fell progressively below standard growth curves with disproportionately short arms and legs, while his head size and facial features remained normal.

Physical examination reveals a short trunk with disproportionately short, broad upper and lower limbs. The child has bowing deformities of the legs and a waddling gait. Joint laxity is evident in the wrists, knees, and ankles, along with limited range of motion in the hips and elbows. The spine shows mild scoliosis and lumbar lordosis. There is tenderness and occasional swelling around large joints suggestive of early osteoarthritis. The hands and feet are broad with shortened fingers and toes. Muscle strength and neurological exam are normal. Cognitive development is appropriate for age.

Radiographic evaluation demonstrates delayed ossification of the epiphyses with irregular and fragmented epiphyses primarily affecting the hips and knees. Metaphyses of long bones are flared and widened. The pelvis reveals a shallow acetabulum and femoral head irregularities. Carpal bones show delayed ossification. There is no facial dysmorphism or cranial abnormality. Laboratory results including metabolic panel and calcium-phosphate balance are normal.

The clinical presentation is consistent with a rare genetic skeletal dysplasia marked by disproportionate short stature, limb shortening, joint laxity, early arthrosis, and characteristic radiologic findings affecting the epiphyses and metaphyses of long bones. Multidisciplinary supportive care including orthopedics and physical therapy is planned. Genetic counseling is recommended for the family.
