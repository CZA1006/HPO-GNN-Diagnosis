The patient is a 2-year-old male presenting with generalized skin abnormalities since birth. He was delivered at term after an uncomplicated pregnancy and noted to have thickened, scaly skin in the neonatal period. His parents describe persistent dryness, scaling, and recurrent fissures leading to episodes of skin infection. Family history is notable for a maternal cousin with similar skin findings. On physical examination, the patient demonstrates generalized fine to plate-like scaling, predominantly on the trunk and extremities, sparing the flexural folds. The skin is dry and mildly erythematous, with hyperkeratosis over the knees and elbows. Hair and nails are normal. Laboratory studies, including complete blood count, liver and renal function, and metabolic screening, are normal. Histopathological analysis of a skin biopsy shows hyperkeratosis with marked orthokeratosis and abnormal stratification, supporting a congenital keratinization disorder.
Muenke syndrome (OMIM:602849)

The patient is a 5-year-old female evaluated for abnormal head shape and developmental progression. She was born following a normal pregnancy, with cranial abnormalities identified soon after birth. Family history reveals her father had corrective cranial surgery as a child. On examination, the patient has brachycephaly with coronal craniosynostosis, mild hypertelorism, and midface flattening. Hearing evaluation demonstrates mild bilateral sensorineural hearing loss. Hands show mild brachydactyly and broad thumbs. Developmental milestones have been achieved on time, with normal cognitive function. A cranial CT scan confirms bilateral coronal suture fusion with no evidence of intracranial hypertension. Audiological testing corroborates the hearing deficit. Other systemic evaluations are within normal limits.
Acromesomelic dysplasia 1, Maroteaux type (OMIM:602875)

The patient is an 8-year-old boy presenting with disproportionate short stature. Pregnancy and delivery were unremarkable. His growth plateaued around age 2, and his parents noticed progressively shortened forearms and lower legs. Family history reveals no known skeletal conditions. On examination, the child displays severe short stature (<3rd percentile) with disproportionately short forearms, hands, lower legs, and feet. Facial appearance includes mild frontal bossing and depressed nasal bridge. Clinical evaluation shows normal intelligence and absence of visceral organ involvement. Radiographic skeletal survey demonstrates rhizomelic and mesomelic shortening, metaphyseal irregularity, and cone-shaped epiphyses in the hands. Laboratory studies including calcium, phosphate, vitamin D, and parathyroid hormone were within normal limits.
Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, familial, 2 (OMIM:603553)

The patient is a 6-month-old male admitted with persistent high fevers, hepatosplenomegaly, and pancytopenia. He was born at term without complications. Family history reveals that an older sibling died in infancy from a febrile illness of unclear cause. On examination, the child is febrile, pale, with marked hepatosplenomegaly and generalized lymphadenopathy. Laboratory evaluation shows anemia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia. Ferritin is markedly elevated, triglycerides are high, and fibrinogen is low. Bone marrow aspiration demonstrates numerous activated macrophages showing hemophagocytosis. Infectious screening is negative. Coagulation testing reveals evidence of evolving disseminated intravascular coagulation. Imaging studies confirm hepatosplenomegaly without focal lesions.
Acromelic frontonasal dysostosis (OMIM:603671)

The patient is a newborn female referred for craniofacial anomalies observed at birth. Pregnancy was uncomplicated, but prenatal ultrasounds suggested abnormal midline development. Family history is negative for congenital anomalies. On physical examination, the infant shows hypertelorism, wide nasal bridge with a midline nasal groove, anterior cranium bifidum with frontal encephalocele, and clefting involving the upper lip. Limb examination reveals shortened distal extremities with abnormal finger morphology. Cardiac and abdominal ultrasound studies show no internal malformations. Brain MRI identifies a small frontal encephalocele and bifid frontal malformation without hydrocephalus. Skeletal imaging reveals shortening of the hands and feet consistent with acromelic changes.
