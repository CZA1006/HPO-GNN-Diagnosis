Ichthyosis, congenital, autosomal recessive 11 (OMIM:602400)

The patient is a 2-year-old male presenting with generalized skin abnormalities since birth. He was delivered at term after an uncomplicated pregnancy and noted to have thickened, scaly skin in the neonatal period. His parents describe persistent dryness, scaling, and recurrent fissures leading to episodes of skin infection. Family history is notable for a maternal cousin with similar skin findings. On physical examination, the patient demonstrates generalized fine to plate-like scaling, predominantly on the trunk and extremities, sparing the flexural folds. The skin is dry and mildly erythematous, with hyperkeratosis over the knees and elbows. Hair and nails are normal. Laboratory studies, including complete blood count, liver and renal function, and metabolic screening, are normal. Histopathological analysis of a skin biopsy shows hyperkeratosis with marked orthokeratosis and abnormal stratification, supporting a congenital keratinization disorder.
