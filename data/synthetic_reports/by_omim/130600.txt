A 12-year-old male presents with episodes of fatigue, mild jaundice, and intermittent paleness. He has a history of easy bruising and occasional shortness of breath during physical activity. The family history is significant for several relatives with similar blood abnormalities, some having undergone splenectomy. The child has no history of severe anemia or transfusion dependence.

On physical examination, mild pallor and slight scleral icterus are noted. The spleen is palpable just below the left costal margin, indicating mild splenomegaly. There are no signs of lymphadenopathy or hepatomegaly. Neurological and cardiovascular exams are normal.

Complete blood count reveals mild anemia with reticulocytosis. Peripheral blood smear demonstrates a high proportion of elliptically shaped red blood cells (elliptocytes), with some ovalocytes present. Osmotic fragility tests are normal or mildly increased. Other hemolysis markers, such as elevated lactate dehydrogenase and indirect bilirubin, are noted during symptom flares. No evidence of spherocytosis or other red cell morphological abnormalities is found.

Further investigations exclude other causes of hemolytic anemia, and red cell membrane protein analysis suggests defects in spectrin or related membrane proteins. Genetic testing is pending.

This clinical picture is compatible with an inherited hemolytic anemia characterized by elliptically shaped red cells, variable hemolysis, splenomegaly, and mild anemia, pending genetic confirmation.
