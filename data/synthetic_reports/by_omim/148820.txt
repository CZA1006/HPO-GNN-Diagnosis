A 5-year-old male presents with congenital sensorineural hearing loss and distinctive pigmentation abnormalities. The parents report that he was born with unusually patchy areas of hypopigmentation on his skin and white patches in his hair. He has low-set eyebrows that tend to grow together (synophrys), and the eyes show differences in iris color between both eyes. Facial examination reveals a broad nasal root with hypoplastic nasal alae, downslanting palpebral fissures, and dystopia canthorum characterized by lateral displacement of the inner corners of the eyes.

Musculoskeletal evaluation shows abnormalities predominantly affecting the upper limbs, including hypoplasia of the muscles and bones of the hands and wrists, flexion contractures of the fingers, fusion of carpal bones, and syndactyly. There is noted limited mobility in the wrists and fingers due to joint stiffness and contractures. The child also has mild developmental delay but normal muscle tone and strength without major neurologic deficits.

Audiological tests confirm bilateral sensorineural hearing loss. No cleft lip or palate is present. There are no gastrointestinal or cardiac anomalies noted. Laboratory studies are within normal limits. Radiographic imaging demonstrates fusion of carpal bones and hypoplastic upper limb bones. Genetic testing is pending.

This constellation of clinical features includes sensorineural deafness, pigmentary abnormalities of the hair, eyes, and skin, dystopia canthorum, and significant musculoskeletal anomalies of the upper limbs, characteristic of a syndromic disorder affecting multiple systems.
