A 28-year-old male presents with gradually worsening kidney function over several years, discovered incidentally during routine blood tests. The patient reports increased thirst, frequent urination including nocturia, and occasional episodes of gout characterized by sudden, severe pain and swelling in the joints, particularly the big toe. There is no significant proteinuria or hematuria noted on urine tests. The patient denies symptoms of infection, hematuria, or edema. Past medical history is unremarkable for hypertension or diabetes.

Family history reveals several relatives with chronic kidney disease presenting in middle age, some requiring dialysis. Physical examination is unremarkable except for mild tenderness in the affected joints. Blood tests show elevated serum creatinine and blood urea nitrogen consistent with chronic kidney disease stage 3. Serum uric acid levels are elevated. Electrolyte levels are within normal limits. Urinalysis reveals bland sediment with absent or minimal proteinuria. Kidney ultrasound demonstrates normal size to mildly reduced size kidneys without cysts or obstruction.

A kidney biopsy was performed, which showed interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy with thickening of tubular basement membranes, consistent with chronic tubulointerstitial nephropathy. No evidence of glomerulonephritis or immune complex deposition was seen.

This clinical course is characterized by autosomal dominant inheritance of progressive tubulointerstitial kidney disease with hyperuricemia, gout, bland urinary sediment, and gradual progression to end-stage kidney disease typically in the fourth to sixth decade of life. Further diagnostic studies including genetic testing are planned.
