Spastic paraplegia 10, autosomal dominant (OMIM:604187)

The patient is a 28-year-old male presenting with progressive lower limb stiffness and weakness over the past two years. He reports difficulty walking long distances and frequent tripping. Family history reveals a father with similar symptoms beginning in middle age. On neurological examination, there is increased tone (spasticity) in the lower limbs with hyperreflexia and bilateral extensor plantar responses. Muscle strength is mildly reduced in the lower extremities, and gait is characterized by scissoring and spasticity. Sensory examination is normal. Brain and spinal MRI are unremarkable without evidence of demyelination or compressive lesions. Nerve conduction studies and electromyography show no peripheral neuropathy. Routine blood tests including vitamin B12 and thyroid function are normal.
