A 22-year-old male presents with gradually declining kidney function, initially detected during routine screening due to a positive family history of kidney disease affecting multiple first-degree relatives. The patient reports mild fatigue but no significant symptoms of fluid retention, swelling, or infections. He mentions increased thirst and nocturia but denies pain or hematuria. There is no history of hypertension or diabetes. Occasionally, he experiences joint discomfort and has a history of gout starting in late adolescence.

Physical examination is unremarkable with normal vital signs and no peripheral edema. Laboratory studies show elevated serum creatinine indicating stage 3 chronic kidney disease, with normal or mildly elevated blood urea nitrogen. Serum uric acid is elevated. Electrolytes and acid-base status are within normal limits. Urinalysis reveals bland sediment with no proteinuria or hematuria. Renal ultrasound demonstrates normal-sized kidneys with no cysts or obstructions.

No extrarenal manifestations such as diabetes or liver disease are evident. Family history is significant for autosomal dominant inheritance of kidney disease resulting in renal replacement therapy in relatives aged 40-60 years. Kidney biopsy in an affected family member shows marked tubulointerstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy without significant glomerular abnormalities or immune complex deposition.

This clinical presentation is of slowly progressive tubulointerstitial nephropathy with autosomal dominant inheritance, bland urinary sediment, hyperuricemia with gout episodes, and progression to end-stage renal disease in middle age. Genetic testing will be essential for definitive diagnosis and family counseling.
