A newborn male is brought to clinic a few days after birth due to the presence of extra fingers on both hands. On examination, the child has a fully developed extra digit on the ulnar side of each hand; both digits articulate with the fifth metacarpal and have well-formed nails and normal skin creases. The digits move passively but lack voluntary movement and seem nonfunctional. The extra digits are of similar length and width to the other fingers. The feet have no abnormalities. No other syndromic features are observed.

The family notes that several relatives on the paternal side, including the father and paternal aunt, were born with similar extra digits, which were surgically removed in childhood. None of these relatives displayed any other health concerns. Developmental milestones are appropriate for age. Physical examination is otherwise normal, with no other malformations or dysmorphic features. There are no signs of syndactyly, short stature, or heart murmurs.

Radiographs confirm that each extra digit contains bony structures and is articulated with the fifth metacarpal. There is no evidence of skeletal or cardiac abnormality. Laboratory findings, including metabolic panel and thyroid function, are normal. Neonatal cranial and abdominal ultrasound studies show no anomalies.

This case demonstrates a congenital limb anomaly characterized by the presence of well-formed, extra digits on the ulnar aspect of both hands, a positive family history, and otherwise normal physical and developmental examination findings. There are no signs of associated anomalies, and the extra digits are scheduled for removal by pediatric surgery.
