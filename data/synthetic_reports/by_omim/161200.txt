A 6-year-old female presents with a history of abnormal nail development and joint problems. The parents report small, thin, and split thumbnails evident since infancy, with less severe changes in other fingernails and toenails. The nails are often ridged, discolored, and occasionally cracked. The child has frequent patellar (kneecap) dislocations and difficulty fully extending her knees, causing episodic knee pain and instability. Examination reveals small or absent kneecaps and a tendency for the legs to bow outward (genu varum). The elbows show limited motion with mild contractures and abnormal skin webbing at the flexural creases, limiting arm extension and rotation.

Radiographic imaging identifies the presence of bilateral iliac horns, which are bony projections from the pelvic bones, considered pathognomonic for this syndrome. The hips appear shallow, and mild scoliosis is evident. Ophthalmologic examination reveals increased intraocular pressure, raising concern for early glaucoma. Renal function tests show no current impairment, but monitoring is ongoing given the risk for nephropathy. The patient has no neurological deficits but reports occasional hand numbness and tingling.

This clinical presentation is characteristic of a multisystem genetic disorder affecting nails, skeletal system (especially knees, elbows, pelvis), eyes, and kidneys. Genetic testing is recommended for definitive diagnosis.
