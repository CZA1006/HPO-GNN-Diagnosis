A 5-month-old male infant, born at term to healthy, unrelated parents, presents with a history of recurrent, severe infections involving the respiratory tract and oral candidiasis since the first few weeks of life. The patient exhibits failure to thrive, poor weight gain, and delayed motor milestones. There is no family history of immunodeficiency or consanguinity. The infant has needed multiple hospitalizations for pneumonia and otitis media. Parents report persistent diarrhea and skin rashes. No history of vaccine reactions is documented.

On physical examination, the infant appears cachectic and lethargic. Vital signs reveal intermittent low-grade fever and mild tachypnea. Lymphoid tissues including tonsils and lymph nodes are markedly small or absent. There is generalized hypotonia and delayed developmental responses appropriate for age. Skin shows scattered eczema-like lesions. No hepatosplenomegaly is detected. Cardiac and abdominal exams are unremarkable.

Laboratory evaluation shows profoundly decreased lymphocyte counts affecting T, B, and natural killer cell lineages. Immunoglobulin levels are severely reduced in all isotypes. Blood film and bone marrow studies do not show malignancy but reveal lymphopenia. Enzyme assays demonstrate markedly reduced adenosine deaminase activity in erythrocytes. Imaging studies reveal no congenital anomalies. Genetic tests have not yet been performed.

The clinical picture is consistent with a severe combined immunodeficiency syndrome, characterized by profound immunodeficiency, multiple infections, failure to thrive, and enzyme deficiency, pending genetic confirmation.
