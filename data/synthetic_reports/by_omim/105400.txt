A 52-year-old male presents with progressive muscle weakness and wasting that began insidiously about one year ago. Initial symptoms included difficulty with fine motor tasks such as buttoning clothes and occasional muscle cramps in both hands. He has since developed weakness in his legs, causing frequent tripping and difficulty walking. The patient reports occasional muscle twitching and stiffness. He also describes slurred speech and mild difficulty swallowing.

On examination, there is marked muscle wasting and weakness predominantly in the distal upper limbs and lower limbs. Fasciculations are observed in the tongue and limbs. There is increased muscle tone with spasticity and brisk tendon reflexes in the limbs. Babinski sign is present bilaterally. Sensory examination is normal. Cognitive function remains intact without behavioral changes.

Laboratory tests including routine blood work are unremarkable. Electromyography and nerve conduction studies demonstrate findings consistent with combined upper and lower motor neuron involvement. Brain and spine MRI show no significant abnormalities. Pulmonary function tests indicate mild respiratory muscle weakness. Genetic testing has not been performed yet. This clinical picture suggests a progressive neurodegenerative disorder affecting motor neurons that controls voluntary muscle movement, with no definitive diagnosis at present.
