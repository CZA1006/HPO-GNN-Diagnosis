A 5-year-old female child is brought in for evaluation due to feeding difficulties and failure to thrive noted since infancy. The parents report she was born with a broad forehead and widely spaced eyes, which are slightly droopy. Physical examination reveals distinctive facial features including hypertelorism, downward slanting palpebral fissures with ptosis, a depressed nasal bridge with a broad nose tip, and low-set, thick, posteriorly rotated ears. The child also has a short neck with excess skin folds and a low posterior hairline. Curly hair texture is noted.

Cardiac examination detects a systolic murmur, and echocardiography reveals pulmonary valve stenosis with mild pulmonic valve dysplasia. Growth parameters are below the 10th percentile with delayed motor milestones. Skeletal examination shows a broad chest with sternal deformity and mild scoliosis. There is mild hypotonia without any neurological deficits. Ophthalmological evaluation finds strabismus and refractive errors. Audiological screening notes mild hearing loss.

Laboratory investigations including metabolic and thyroid panels are unremarkable. Developmental assessment indicates mild speech delay and learning difficulties. No evidence of infection or systemic illness is present. Family history reveals a paternal uncle with short stature and congenital heart disease.

This clinical picture suggests a multisystem developmental disorder with characteristic craniofacial abnormalities, congenital heart defect, growth retardation, skeletal anomalies, visual and hearing impairments, and mild neurodevelopmental delay. Further genetic testing is planned for diagnostic confirmation and family counseling.
