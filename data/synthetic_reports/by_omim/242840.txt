A 6-month-old male infant is referred for developmental delay, failure to thrive, and recurrent respiratory infections. His parents report poor feeding since birth and difficulty maintaining weight despite supplementation. He has exhibited profound hypotonia and exhibits little spontaneous movement. Multiple hospitalizations for pneumonia and otitis media have occurred. No family history of similar conditions is reported.

On examination, the infant has marked growth retardation with weight and length below the 5th percentile. He demonstrates global hypotonia with absent deep tendon reflexes. Facial features include a prominent forehead, broad nasal bridge, and micrognathia. Ophthalmologic evaluation reveals bilateral cataracts. Brain MRI shows agenesis of the corpus callosum and cerebral atrophy. Laboratory investigations show progressive immunodeficiency with low lymphocyte counts and hypogammaglobulinemia.
