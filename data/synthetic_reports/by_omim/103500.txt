A newborn female infant is brought for evaluation due to profound bilateral hearing loss identified shortly after birth. She has strikingly pale skin, white to light blond hair, and white eyebrows and eyelashes. The parents report no other health concerns and normal prenatal history. There are no other affected family members, and the pregnancy was unremarkable. Psychomotor development appears normal so far.

On physical examination, the infant exhibits generalized hypopigmentation involving the skin, hair, and eyes. The iris is noted to be blue, and there is a lack of pigmentation in the retinal pigment epithelial cells evident on specialized ophthalmologic exam. Hearing tests confirm congenital profound sensorineural hearing loss in both ears. No dysmorphic facial features or structural abnormalities are observed. Growth parameters are within normal limits.

Laboratory evaluations including routine metabolic and genetic screening have not yet been completed. Imaging studies and further diagnostic assessments are pending. Genetic testing specifically targeting mutations in pigment-related genes has not been performed yet.

This clinical presentation is characterized by early-onset profound deafness and generalized hypopigmentation of skin, hair, and eyes, with no other systemic abnormalities identified at present.
