A 5-year-old male is evaluated in the dermatology and ophthalmology clinic for very light skin pigmentation and visual problems. Parents report that since birth, the child’s skin and hair have been noticeably lighter than those of other family members. He burns easily with minimal sun exposure despite careful use of sunscreen. Over the past year, his preschool teachers have also expressed concern that he struggles to see objects clearly from a distance and often tilts his head when focusing.

The child was born at term after an uncomplicated pregnancy and delivery. Birth weight and early growth parameters were normal. Motor development has been age-appropriate, though visual challenges have affected his participation in group activities. He has no history of recurrent infections or chronic medical conditions. Family history is notable for both parents having darker skin tones typical of their ethnic background, though the mother recalls a paternal uncle with very light hair and poor eyesight.

On examination, the child has very fair skin with scattered freckles on sun-exposed areas. Hair and eyebrows are light yellow-blond in color, and eyelashes appear pale. Ophthalmological exam reveals reduced visual acuity (20/100 bilaterally), horizontal nystagmus, and strabismus. The iris is light blue and transilluminates under slit-lamp examination. Fundoscopy shows hypopigmentation of the retinal pigment epithelium and macular hypoplasia. Skin examination demonstrates no lesions other than mild actinic changes. Neurological, cardiovascular, and musculoskeletal exams are unremarkable.

Laboratory studies, including complete blood count, metabolic panel, and immune function testing, are within normal limits. Ophthalmologic imaging with optical coherence tomography confirms underdeveloped foveal architecture. Visual evoked potentials reveal misrouting of optic nerve fibers. Dermatologic follow-up emphasizes strict photoprotection due to increased risk of sun-induced skin damage.

The phenotype of generalized hypopigmentation, visual abnormalities, and ocular findings suggests a congenital pigment disorder, and genetic testing is being pursued to establish the definitive cause.
