A 3-year-old female infant presents with hypotonia and delayed motor milestones, including delayed sitting and walking. Parents report stiff joints, especially in the fingers, wrists, and ankles, which have progressively worsened over the past year. The child exhibits joint hyperlaxity in some areas but also develops contractures that limit movement, particularly in the long finger flexors and Achilles tendons. She experiences mild muscle weakness primarily in the proximal limb muscles, and has difficulty climbing stairs and rising from a seated position.

Physical examination shows characteristic contractures affecting the elbows, wrists, fingers, hips, knees, and ankles. The child's skin is soft and velvety with evidence of follicular hyperkeratosis, and she has some atrophic 'cigarette paper' scars on the forearms. Muscle bulk is mildly reduced, with no signs of pseudohypertrophy. Respiratory function is normal so far but is being monitored due to concerns of late-onset respiratory weakness. No cardiac abnormalities are detected.

Electromyography indicates myopathic changes with fiber size variability and internal nuclei. Serum creatine kinase levels are normal to mildly elevated. Muscle biopsy confirms nonspecific myopathic changes. Radiological and neurological assessments exclude other central nervous system involvement. The condition shows slow progression with ambulatory function usually preserved into adulthood.

The clinical features suggest a congenital muscular dystrophy characterized by early-onset muscle weakness, joint contractures, skin abnormalities, and slow progression without significant cardiac or respiratory compromise at this stage. Genetic testing has not yet been performed.
