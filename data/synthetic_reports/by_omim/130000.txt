A 7-year-old female presents with a history of unusually flexible joints and fragile, stretchy skin noted since infancy. The parents report frequent joint dislocations and subluxations, particularly of the shoulders, knees, and fingers. There is a history of easy bruising and wounds that heal slowly, often leaving thin, atrophic scars resembling cigarette paper. The skin appears very soft and velvety to touch, with some areas showing mild hyperextensibility, especially over the elbows and knees. The child also exhibits molluscoid pseudotumors—small, fleshy lesions over pressure points—and piezogenic papules on the feet. Fatigue and mild muscle weakness have been reported but no major systemic complications.

Physical examination confirms significant generalized joint hypermobility with hyperextension of several large and small joints. Skin elasticity is increased but returns to normal quickly when stretched. Multiple atrophic scars are visible, primarily on the forehead, shins, and knees. The facial appearance includes prominent eyes, thin nose, and thin lips, along with some mild dental crowding. Cardiovascular examination is normal without evidence of valvular abnormalities. Neurological and ophthalmological evaluations are unremarkable.

Laboratory tests including coagulation profiles, inflammatory markers, and metabolic panels are normal. Radiologic studies show early signs of joint degeneration but no bone deformities. Genetic testing is pending.

This clinical presentation is consistent with a heritable connective tissue disorder marked by skin hyperextensibility, atrophic scarring, generalized joint hypermobility, and associated complications, pending molecular confirmation.
