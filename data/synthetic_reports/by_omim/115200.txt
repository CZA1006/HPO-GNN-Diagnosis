A 7-year-old male child presents with progressive symptoms of exertional breathlessness, fatigue, and decreased exercise tolerance over the past several months. Parents report episodes of poor feeding and difficulty gaining weight. There has been increased irritability and occasional swelling of the lower legs. The child has no significant past medical history but there is a family history of heart disease with a paternal uncle who died suddenly in adulthood.

On physical examination, the child appears pale and mildly edematous in the lower extremities. Vital signs reveal tachycardia and mild respiratory distress. Cardiovascular examination detects a displaced and diffuse apex beat with a soft systolic murmur consistent with mitral regurgitation. Jugular venous distension is noted with hepatomegaly but no ascites. Lung auscultation reveals bibasilar crackles. Peripheral pulses are diminished, and capillary refill time is prolonged. Muscle tone and neurological exams are normal.

Laboratory investigations reveal elevated brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) and mild elevation of liver enzymes. Chest X-ray shows cardiomegaly with pulmonary congestion. Echocardiogram demonstrates a dilated left ventricle with markedly reduced systolic function and global hypokinesia. No structural valve abnormalities are seen except mild mitral regurgitation. Electrocardiogram reveals sinus tachycardia with nonspecific conduction abnormalities. No arrhythmias are recorded. Genetic testing has not yet been performed.
