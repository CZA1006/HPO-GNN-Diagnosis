A 2-year-old female presents with noticeable growth delay and developmental delay. She was born after an uneventful pregnancy to unrelated parents, with a low birth weight and small head circumference. The parents report feeding difficulties in infancy, frequent regurgitation, and episodes of severe vomiting. Physical examination reveals distinctive facial features including thick, arched eyebrows that often grow together (synophrys), long curly eyelashes, a small and upturned nose with anteverted nostrils, a smooth and long philtrum, thin downturned lips, low-set ears, and a small jaw (micrognathia). The child has a low hairline and excess body hair (hypertrichosis). She also exhibits small hands and feet with tapered fingers, inward deviation of the fifth fingers (clinodactyly), and partial webbing (syndactyly) of some toes. Oral examination reveals widely spaced, small teeth, with some dental anomalies.

Neurologic evaluation shows mild intellectual disability with delays in speech and motor milestones. Hearing tests reveal mild sensorineural hearing loss. There is evidence of gastroesophageal reflux and recurrent respiratory infections. Cardiac evaluation identifies structural abnormalities such as pulmonary stenosis. Skeletal imaging demonstrates delayed bone age and abnormalities including scoliosis and short ribs. Vision screening is notable for refractive errors but no major ocular malformations. Laboratory tests are within normal limits. Genetic testing has not been performed.

This complex clinical picture involving distinctive craniofacial appearance, growth delays, limb anomalies, cardiac defects, and developmental challenges is suggestive of an autosomal dominant multisystem disorder, pending confirmatory genetic analysis
