A 14-month-old male infant presents with marked growth failure and poor weight gain noted since the first year of life. The child was born after an uncomplicated pregnancy and showed normal appearance at birth. However, within the first year, his parents observed that his growth was disproportionately slow compared to other children. On physical examination, the infant has a disproportionately large head relative to his small, narrow face. Distinct facial features include a beaked nose, thin lips, small mouth with delayed and incomplete dentition, micrognathia, and prominent eyes with incomplete closure of the eyelids.

The scalp hair, eyebrows, and eyelashes are markedly sparse, progressing towards alopecia. The skin appears unusually thin, dry, and wrinkled, with prominent veins visible over the scalp and extremities. Areas of shiny, taut skin with dimpling and small irregular outpouchings are noted on the abdomen and thighs. Multiple fingernails and toenails appear dystrophic, thin, and brittle. The child shows limited joint mobility with progressive contractures affecting knees, elbows, and fingers.

Radiological evaluation reveals delayed closure of the anterior fontanelle, a narrow, pear-shaped chest with small, thin ribs and clavicles, and evidence of osteolysis of terminal phalanges. Joint stiffness and contractures cause a characteristic wide-based gait when ambulatory. Cardiovascular assessment identifies signs of early arteriosclerosis with increased arterial stiffness; EKG and echocardiogram show no structural abnormalities but close monitoring is planned. The child has no cognitive impairment and developmental milestones are age appropriate except for delayed motor skills due to joint limitations.

Laboratory studies reveal no metabolic abnormalities; serum calcium, phosphate, and parathyroid hormone levels are within normal limits. No evidence of infection or inflammation is present. The clinical features characterize a rare, progressive genetic condition resulting in premature aging appearance and multisystem involvement predominantly affecting skin, bones, joints, and cardiovascular system. Multidisciplinary care and further monitoring are underway.
