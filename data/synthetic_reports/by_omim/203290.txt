A 7-year-old female is referred for evaluation of unusually light hair and skin tone compared to her immediate family. Her parents report that since birth, she has had reddish-brown hair and lighter skin than her siblings, though she does not sunburn as easily as children with very fair complexions in her community. They became more concerned after noticing she often squints in bright light and has difficulty following the board at school unless seated at the front. Despite these visual concerns, her growth and development have otherwise been normal.

She was born at term with no perinatal complications, and developmental milestones were achieved appropriately. Medical history is unremarkable, with no recurrent infections or hospitalizations. Family history is notable for relatives with darker skin, hair, and eye pigmentation typical of their ethnic background. There is no known family history of vision disorders.

On physical examination, the child has light brown skin with freckles evident on sun-exposed areas, particularly the face and forearms. Hair is reddish-brown with coppery tones, while eyebrows and eyelashes are lighter than average but not white. Ophthalmological exam reveals reduced visual acuity (20/80 bilaterally), mild photophobia, and pendular nystagmus. The irises are hazel with partial transillumination. Fundoscopy demonstrates hypopigmentation of the retinal pigment epithelium and reduced macular definition. Overall skin exam shows no actinic keratoses or precancerous lesions at this stage. Cardiac, neurologic, and musculoskeletal examinations are normal.

Laboratory testing, including blood counts, metabolic panel, and immune markers, are within normal ranges. Ophthalmologic imaging with optical coherence tomography shows mild foveal hypoplasia. Visual evoked potential testing indicates subtle abnormalities in optic nerve signal routing. Dermatology consultation emphasizes the importance of sun protection due to increased lifetime skin cancer risk.

The combination of reduced but not absent pigmentation in skin and hair, along with ocular changes and visual impairment, points toward a congenital pigmentation disorder of intermediate severity, for which genetic analysis is being arranged to provide confirmation.
