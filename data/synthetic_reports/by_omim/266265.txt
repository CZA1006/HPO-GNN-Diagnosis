A 2-year-old female presents with developmental delay, recurrent infections, and distinctive facial features. Parents report failure to gain appropriate milestones, frequent respiratory infections, and feeding difficulties.

On examination, the child has coarse facial features, broad nasal bridge, and inverted nipples. Neurologic exam reveals hypotonia and poor motor coordination. Laboratory analysis shows abnormal serum transferrin glycosylation patterns. Immunoglobulin levels are low, and there is evidence of neutropenia. Cardiac and abdominal ultrasounds are normal.
