A 35-year-old male presents with premature aging features and a history of diabetes mellitus. He reports early graying of hair, bilateral cataracts diagnosed in his late twenties, and progressive skin tightening. There is a history of non-healing skin ulcers and recent myocardial infarction. Family history is negative for similar symptoms.

Physical examination shows thin, atrophic skin with areas of pigmentation changes and telangiectasia. The patient has a beaked nose, tight skin on the hands and feet, and short stature. Neurological and cardiovascular exams reveal diminished peripheral pulses and mild neuropathy. Laboratory tests indicate insulin resistance, elevated cholesterol, and osteoporosis on bone density scan. Genetic studies are being considered to confirm the diagnosis.
