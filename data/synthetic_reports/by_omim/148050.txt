A 7-year-old male presents with mild developmental delay and learning difficulties. He has distinctive facial features including a broad, triangular-shaped face, widely spaced eyes with synophrys (connected eyebrows), a prominent nasal bridge with a bulbous tip, and large, prominent ears. The upper front teeth are unusually large (macrodontia), particularly noticeable with the central incisors. He has short stature and shows mild skeletal abnormalities such as short, broad fingers with clinodactyly of the fifth fingers, delayed bone age, and mild scoliosis.

The child also has a history of speech delay and behavioral issues such as attention deficit and anxiety. Hearing assessments reveal mild hearing loss likely related to recurrent ear infections. There is no significant muscle weakness or neurological deficits. Growth parameters are below average for his age, but weight is appropriate. There are mild feeding difficulties reported during infancy.

Physical examination confirms the facial dysmorphisms and skeletal findings, with no organomegaly or other abnormalities noted. Neurological exam shows normal tone and reflexes. Routine laboratory tests are within normal limits. Brain imaging has not revealed major abnormalities.

This clinical presentation suggests a complex developmental disorder with craniofacial, dental, skeletal, cognitive, and behavioral features requiring further genetic evaluation.
