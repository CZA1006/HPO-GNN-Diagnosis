A 17-year-old female presents with progressive thinning of subcutaneous fat, first noticed around puberty, particularly in the limbs and trunk. The patient reports a change in body shape with a loss of fat from the arms and legs, leading to a muscular appearance despite a normal or slightly increased body weight. Fat accumulation is noted in the face, neck, and abdominal region, giving a cushingoid appearance. There is history of insulin resistance, with recent diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus managed with oral hypoglycemic agents. She also experiences episodes of muscle pain and weakness.

Physical examination confirms loss of fat in the distal and proximal limbs and trunk with preservation or increase of fat in the face and neck. Prominent veins are visible over the limbs, reflecting subcutaneous fat loss. The patient’s height and weight are within normal ranges, but body fat distribution is markedly abnormal. Skin is without lesions but shows mild acanthosis nigricans in body folds.

Laboratory evaluations reveal hypertriglyceridemia, low HDL cholesterol, elevated fasting glucose and insulin levels consistent with insulin resistance. Liver function tests are abnormal with mild elevation of transaminases, and abdominal ultrasound shows evidence of fatty liver disease. Cardiovascular assessment identifies early signs of atherosclerosis. Reproductive history reveals oligomenorrhea with signs consistent with polycystic ovary syndrome including hirsutism and infertility concerns.

The clinical presentation suggests a multisystem metabolic disorder characterized by partial loss of adipose tissue, fat redistribution, associated metabolic complications including diabetes, dyslipidemia, fatty liver, and reproductive abnormalities. Genetic testing has not been performed yet.
