A 6-month-old male infant presents with pallor, feeding difficulties, and irritability noted since early infancy. He was born at term with no perinatal complications and is the first child of unrelated healthy parents. The parents report poor weight gain and episodes of rapid heartbeat and increased sleepiness. There is no family history of anemia or other hematologic disorders. The infant has not had any significant infections or bleeding episodes.

On physical examination, the infant appears pale and mildly undernourished. Growth parameters are below the 5th percentile for age. There are noted subtle dysmorphic features including a small head circumference (microcephaly), wide-set eyes, a flat nasal bridge, low-set ears, and a small lower jaw. Limb examination reveals shortened thumb bilaterally and mild webbing of the neck. No hepatosplenomegaly is present. Cardiovascular and respiratory examinations are within normal limits.

Laboratory investigations reveal severe macrocytic anemia with markedly reduced reticulocytes. White blood cell and platelet counts are within normal ranges. Serum erythrocyte adenosine deaminase (eADA) activity is elevated. Bone marrow aspiration shows a paucity of erythroid precursors with otherwise normal cellularity. Imaging studies exclude major congenital anomalies. Genetic testing is pending.
