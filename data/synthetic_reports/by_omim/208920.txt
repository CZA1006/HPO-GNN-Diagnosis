A 9-year-old male presents with progressive difficulty walking, which started around age 5. Parents noticed frequent falls, poor coordination, and head thrusting movements when trying to follow objects visually. School reports highlight learning difficulties and fatigue. He has also developed drooping of the eyelids and requires assistance with climbing stairs.

On physical examination, the child has truncal and limb ataxia, scanning speech, and impaired horizontal eye movements with compensatory head thrusting consistent with oculomotor apraxia. There is evidence of distal muscle weakness and areflexia in lower limbs. Laboratory studies reveal hypoalbuminemia and hypercholesterolemia. Nerve conduction studies demonstrate axonal sensorimotor neuropathy. Brain MRI shows cerebellar atrophy.
