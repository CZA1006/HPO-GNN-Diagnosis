A 2-year-old male presents with multiple congenital anomalies involving the face, hands, and forearms. The parents report feeding difficulties and episodes of noisy breathing since birth. The child has a small lower jaw (micrognathia) that contributes to airway obstruction and poor suckling. Facial features include downward slanting of the eyes (palpebral fissures), absent or notched lower eyelids (colobomas), deficiency of eyelashes in the medial portion of the lower eyelids, and underdeveloped cheekbones (malar hypoplasia). The outer ears are small, malformed, and low-set, with some hearing difficulty suggestive of conductive hearing loss.

Examination of the upper limbs reveals hypoplasia or absence of the thumbs, shortened forearms due to partial or complete absence of the radius bone, and limited elbow extension. Some fingers are curved (clinodactyly), webbed (syndactyly), or fixed in a bent position (camptodactyly). The forearms appear short, and movement is restricted due to radioulnar synostosis or soft tissue contractures. In the lower limbs, mild deformities such as clubfoot or hallux valgus may be present. The spine shows mild scoliosis.

Neurological examination is unremarkable with normal cognition and muscle tone. Imaging confirms mandibular hypoplasia, malar hypoplasia, radius hypoplasia or aplasia, and carpal bone anomalies. Cardiorespiratory evaluation is necessary due to airway concerns. No major cardiac or renal malformations are apparent.

This clinical presentation is consistent with a congenital disorder involving craniofacial and preaxial limb abnormalities resulting in feeding, breathing, hearing, and motor difficulties. Genetic testing and multidisciplinary care are planned for further evaluation and management.
