A 1-year-old male infant is brought in due to localized hard, bumpy skin lesions present since birth that have progressively increased in size and number. The parents describe initially noticing small, rough patches on the trunk and limbs that felt unusually firm. Over the past several months, these patches have coalesced into larger plaques of hardened skin, extending deeper into the subcutaneous tissues. Physical examination reveals multiple subcutaneous nodules and plaques with a rock-hard consistency on the torso and proximal limbs. Some areas show limited joint mobility due to underlying firm plaques restricting movement. The affected skin areas appear coarse, with occasional superficial ulceration.

Radiological studies demonstrate abnormal bone formation within the skin and subcutaneous tissue, extending into deep connective tissues including fascia, skeletal muscle, and tendons, suggestive of heterotopic ossification. There is no evidence of systemic inflammation or metabolic abnormalities on laboratory evaluations, with normal serum calcium, phosphate, parathyroid hormone, and vitamin D levels. Growth parameters are within normal limits, but joint contractures have begun to limit range of motion. Family history is unremarkable for similar lesions.

The clinical presentation is indicative of a rare genetic disorder characterized by progressive ectopic bone formation starting in the skin and advancing into deeper soft tissues, resulting in joint ankylosis and potential growth impairment of involved limbs. Current management focuses on symptom control and preserving joint mobility, as no effective preventative treatment is available.
