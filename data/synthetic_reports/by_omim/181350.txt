A 27-year-old male presents with progressive muscle weakness that began in late adolescence. He reports increasing difficulty with activities requiring upper limb strength, such as lifting objects overhead or opening jars, as well as occasional difficulty climbing stairs. He noticed that his elbows have gradually become stiffer over the past several years, limiting full extension, and says that his ankles feel tight in the mornings. He denies sensory symptoms, such as numbness or tingling.

His past medical history is unremarkable, with no significant illnesses in childhood beyond common infections. He has never undergone surgery and takes no regular medications. He has no known allergies. The patient works as a computer engineer and previously led an active lifestyle, but reports that he has reduced exercise due to fatigue and mobility concerns. Family history is notable: his father developed muscle weakness in his 40s and later required mobility aids, while his paternal aunt experienced sudden cardiac death in her early 50s. The patient does not recall significant neuromuscular issues on the maternal side.

On physical examination, the patient is alert and oriented with normal cognitive function. Limb inspection reveals mild muscle wasting in the upper arms and lower legs. Muscle tone is within normal limits. Strength is reduced in shoulder abduction and hip flexion, graded 4/5, with distal strength preserved. Deep tendon reflexes are present but reduced in the upper limbs. Contractures are evident in the elbows and Achilles tendons, consistent with his complaints of stiffness. Cardiovascular examination reveals a regular pulse but with occasional dropped beats on auscultation. No murmurs are detected. Respiratory and abdominal examinations are unremarkable.

Laboratory testing demonstrates normal serum electrolytes and renal function. CK levels are mildly elevated, approximately twice the upper normal limit. Electrocardiography shows sinus rhythm with frequent premature atrial contractions and prolonged PR interval. Echocardiography reports normal systolic function but mild atrial enlargement. Nerve conduction studies are within normal limits, while electromyography reveals myopathic changes with small, short-duration motor unit potentials in proximal muscles. A standard radiograph of the elbows confirms reduced extension consistent with soft tissue contracture. Pulmonary function testing demonstrates values within the lower range of normal but without significant restriction at this time.

Based on the clinical picture, patient history, and initial investigations, further diagnostic work-up is planned, with genetic testing under consideration to clarify the underlying etiology.
