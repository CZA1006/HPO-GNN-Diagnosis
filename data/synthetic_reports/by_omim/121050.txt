A 4-year-old male child presents with congenital contractures primarily affecting the elbows, knees, hips, and fingers, noticed since birth. The child displays long, slender fingers and toes (arachnodactyly), with some fingers permanently flexed (camptodactyly), leading to difficulty in fine motor tasks. Parents report a slender build with a tall and thin appearance. The outer ears have an unusual “crumpled” appearance characterized by fold abnormalities. The child has developed progressive curvature of the spine (kyphoscoliosis), which has caused some discomfort and postural difficulties. Muscle development appears slightly reduced with mild muscular hypoplasia. Occasional complaints of chest discomfort have been recorded but without significant cardiac findings.

On physical examination, the child’s joints show limited range of motion due to contractures, especially in large joints. The skin is normal in texture with no signs of inflammation. The musculoskeletal system examination reveals kyphoscoliosis and mild pectus deformity. No craniofacial abnormalities are evident. No neurological deficits or cognitive impairments are observed.

Cardiovascular examination is unremarkable, although echocardiography is planned to monitor potential mitral valve prolapse. Radiological studies confirm the presence of kyphoscoliosis and normal bone density. Genetic testing for mutations in the FBN2 gene has not been performed. This clinical picture suggests a heritable connective tissue disorder characterized by congenital joint contractures, arachnodactyly, spinal curvature, and unique ear morphology, awaiting genetic confirmation.
