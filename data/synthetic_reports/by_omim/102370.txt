A 5-year-old female presents with significantly short stature and disproportionately short limbs. She is the second child of non-consanguineous parents with no relevant family history of similar growth delays or skeletal abnormalities. Her birth was at term with normal weight and length. Over the past few years, the parents have noted slow growth compared to peers and difficulty with fine motor tasks. The child has no intellectual disabilities but shows mild facial features that are perceived as slightly different, including a round face, sharply defined eyebrows, long eyelashes, a bulbous nose with upturned nostrils, a long space between the nose and upper lip, and a small mouth with thick lips. Joint stiffness is also reported, with some limitations in movement but no pain. She has had occasional respiratory infections and no history of seizures or other systemic problems.

On physical examination, her height is well below the 3rd percentile, with short hands and feet exhibiting stubby and broad digits. Mild contractures restrict joint mobility, especially in the fingers. The face is mildly dysmorphic as described by the parents, with normal cranial size. Muscle tone is normal. No neurological deficits are present. Cardiac and pulmonary examinations are unremarkable.

Laboratory investigations including metabolic panels and endocrine studies are within normal limits. Radiographic imaging shows delayed bone age relative to chronological age, shortened long bones, cone-shaped epiphyses of the phalanges, and ovoid vertebral bodies. There is no evidence of fractures or bone lesions. No genetic or molecular testing has been completed to date.
