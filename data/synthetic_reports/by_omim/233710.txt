A 5-year-old male presents with a history of recurrent infections, including pneumonia, lymphadenitis, and hepatic abscesses since infancy. The child has been hospitalized multiple times for persistent bacterial and fungal infections requiring intravenous antibiotics. His parents report poor wound healing and frequent fevers with no clear source. Family history is significant for a maternal cousin who died young from severe infections.

On physical examination, the patient is alert but appears chronically ill, with mild hepatosplenomegaly and several healed scars on the skin. Laboratory evaluation reveals leukocytosis with a neutrophilic predominance, elevated inflammatory markers, and mild anemia. Nitroblue tetrazolium testing performed shows markedly reduced oxidative burst activity in neutrophils. Chest radiographs demonstrate patchy infiltrates consistent with recurrent pneumonia.
