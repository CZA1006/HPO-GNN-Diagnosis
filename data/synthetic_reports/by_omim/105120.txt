A 48-year-old male presents with a progressive history of bilateral facial weakness and difficulty swallowing that developed over several years. He reports drooping of the corners of his mouth and difficulty closing his eyes completely. He has also noticed changes in his skin, describing it as loosened and sagging, especially around the face and neck. Vision problems have been noted, including blurry vision and recurrent eye irritation. Family history reveals relatives with similar late-onset facial weakness and skin changes.

On examination, the patient exhibits bilateral facial palsy affecting both upper and lower facial muscles. Skin around the face and neck appears lax and wrinkled, consistent with cutis laxa. Ophthalmological assessment reveals corneal lattice dystrophy with characteristic lattice-like amyloid deposits in the cornea. Sensory examination demonstrates mild distal numbness and decreased vibration sense in the feet. Reflexes are reduced in the lower limbs but preserved elsewhere. No motor weakness or atrophy is observed. Cardiovascular and abdominal examinations are unremarkable.

Laboratory testing includes normal routine blood chemistry and inflammatory markers. Nerve conduction studies indicate sensory neuropathy with slowed conduction velocities. Skin biopsy stained with Congo red confirms amyloid deposits. Genetic testing is pending. Imaging studies exclude structural brain or spinal abnormalities.
