Neuropathy, hereditary sensory and autonomic, type IIB (OMIM:613115)

The patient is a 16-year-old male presenting with sensory loss, recurrent painless ulcers, and autonomic dysfunction involving the lower limbs. The symptoms began insidiously during early adolescence. Family history reveals a similarly affected paternal uncle. Clinical examination shows loss of pain and temperature sensation in a stocking distribution, absent ankle reflexes, and dry, shiny skin on feet. Autonomic features include orthostatic hypotension and anhidrosis. Motor strength is preserved. Nerve conduction studies confirm a sensory neuronopathy with reduced amplitude of sensory action potentials. Skin biopsy demonstrates loss of intraepidermal nerve fibers. Laboratory workup excludes metabolic causes of neuropathy. Management focuses on ulcer prevention, autonomic symptom control, and physical therapy.
