A 6-year-old male presents with noticeable abnormalities in his upper limbs since birth. The parents report that one of his thumbs appears unusually shaped and smaller than normal, while the other hand appears relatively normal. The child has some difficulty with fine motor tasks such as buttoning clothes and grasping small objects. There is asymmetry in the forearm bones with hypoplasia and limited supination and pronation movements. His shoulders appear narrow and sloping. There is no involvement of the lower limbs.

Cardiac evaluation reveals a history of breathlessness on exertion and occasional palpitations. Echocardiography identifies an atrial septal defect of the secundum type and a small ventricular septal defect. Cardiac conduction studies show intermittent heart block with occasional arrhythmias. The patient has no significant history of syncope or heart failure symptoms at present.

Physical examination confirms underdeveloped thumb bones with possible triphalangy on the right hand, and mild contractures around the wrists. Radiological studies reveal abnormal formation and fusion of carpal bones and asymmetric development of the radius. Growth and neurological development are within normal limits. The family history is unremarkable for similar symptoms or cardiac disease.

This constellation of congenital upper extremity skeletal abnormalities combined with structural and conduction heart defects suggests a multisystem developmental disorder involving both limbs and heart, pending further genetic and diagnostic evaluations.
