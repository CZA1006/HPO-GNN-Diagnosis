A 4-year-old female presents with distinctive craniofacial abnormalities and mild feeding difficulties noticed since infancy. She was born after a full-term pregnancy with no complications and has no significant family history of bone disorders. The child’s head shape is elongated (dolichocephaly), and physical features include widely spaced eyes (hypertelorism), a broad nasal bridge, a prominent forehead, and a large lower jaw. The eyes appear somewhat protruded (proptosis). Feeding and breathing difficulties have been reported due to narrowed nasal passages.

On examination, palpation reveals thickened craniofacial bones, leading to a sensation of hardness around the skull and face. Oral examination shows delayed eruption of teeth and some missing teeth. Neurologic examination is unremarkable but hearing assessment shows mild hearing loss. The limbs appear generally normal but radiographs reveal metaphyseal widening with the long bones, particularly in the legs, showing a characteristic “Erlenmeyer flask” deformity. Skull X-rays demonstrate diffuse sclerosis, thickening of cranial bones, narrowing of foramina, and reduced sinus pneumatization.

Laboratory investigations including serum calcium, phosphate, and alkaline phosphatase are within normal limits or slightly elevated. Genetic testing has not been done yet. The clinical and radiologic features suggest a rare, progressive bone disorder characterized by hyperostosis of craniofacial bones and metaphyseal dysplasia of long bones, pending genetic confirmation.
