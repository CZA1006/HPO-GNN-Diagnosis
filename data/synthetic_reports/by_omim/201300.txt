A 14-year-old female is evaluated in the neurology clinic for progressive loss of sensation and frequent painless injuries. Her parents report that since early childhood she has had little response to pain or temperature changes. She has sustained multiple skin lacerations, burns, and foot injuries without complaint, often only noticed after secondary infection develops. Over the past two years, she has developed ulcerations on her feet and hands that heal poorly, along with recurrent osteomyelitis requiring antibiotics. She also has difficulty distinguishing hot from cold objects and sometimes shows unsteady gait, particularly in the dark.

She was born at term following an uncomplicated pregnancy and delivery. Developmental milestones were delayed mildly in gross motor skills, though speech and cognition have remained normal. Past medical history includes recurrent skin and soft tissue infections, dental injuries due to unnoticed trauma, and a fractured toe that went untreated until swelling prompted evaluation. Family history reveals consanguinity between her parents, and an older sibling reportedly died of sepsis following severe leg infections during adolescence.

On physical examination, the patient appears well-nourished but with multiple scars, calluses, and non-healing ulcers over both feet and hands. Sensory testing reveals markedly reduced perception of pain, temperature, and touch in a stocking-and-glove distribution, while vibration and proprioception are preserved. Motor strength is normal in all limbs, but distal muscle wasting is noted. Reflexes are reduced in the ankles and knees. Autonomic findings include dry, scaly skin over the distal extremities and decreased sweating on quantitative sudomotor testing. Cardiovascular, respiratory, and abdominal examinations are normal, and cognition is intact.

Laboratory results show a normal complete blood count, metabolic panel, and inflammatory markers. Nerve conduction studies demonstrate absent or severely reduced sensory nerve action potentials with preserved motor responses. Electromyography reveals no myopathic features. Skin biopsy of the lower leg shows a severe reduction in intraepidermal nerve fibers. Radiographs of the feet demonstrate evidence of recurrent osteomyelitis and early Charcot-type arthropathy in the metatarsals.

The clinical picture of early-onset sensory loss, autonomic involvement, neuropathic injuries, and inheritance pattern has led to referral for genetic testing to confirm the underlying condition.
