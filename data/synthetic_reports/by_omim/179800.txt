A 3-year-old male infant is brought in due to persistent vomiting, failure to thrive, and recurrent episodes of dehydration. The parents report excessive thirst and frequent urination. The child appears tired and weak with poor muscle tone. Birth history was unremarkable, but growth parameters have consistently lagged below the 5th percentile since infancy. No history of infections or hospitalizations is noted.

Physical examination reveals poor weight gain, mild hypotonia, and delayed motor milestones. There is evidence of metabolic acidosis with deep, rapid breathing (Kussmaul respiration). Skeletal examination notes mild bowing of the legs and tenderness over long bones. The child has no dysmorphic features or edema. Neurological exam is normal.

Laboratory studies show a normal anion gap metabolic acidosis with a persistently elevated urine pH (>5.5) despite systemic acidemia. Serum potassium is low (hypokalemia). Blood gas analysis confirms acidemia. Urinalysis reveals hypercalciuria. Renal ultrasound identifies nephrocalcinosis and small kidney stones. Bone X-rays show signs of rickets with metaphyseal widening and fraying. Hearing screening is normal.

Further investigations exclude other systemic diseases, and autoimmune panels are negative. The family history is non-contributory. Supportive treatment with alkali therapy and potassium supplementation has been initiated. The clinical presentation is characteristic of a rare inherited disorder involving defective acid secretion by the distal renal tubules, leading to systemic metabolic acidosis, electrolyte disturbances, kidney calcifications, and bone abnormalities. Close monitoring and multidisciplinary care are ongoing.
