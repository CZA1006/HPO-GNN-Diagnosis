A 10-year-old female presents with a long-standing history of hypermobile joints and skin abnormalities. She frequently experiences joint dislocations and subluxations, especially in the shoulders, knees, and fingers. The parents note that her skin is unusually soft, highly elastic, and fragile, bruising easily even after minor trauma. Wounds tend to heal slowly and often leave thin, wide, atrophic scars with a paper-like texture. The child exhibits multiple small fleshy nodules over the elbows and knees called molluscoid pseudotumors, as well as piezogenic papules (fat herniations) on the heels. She complains of chronic joint pain and fatigue but has no major systemic complications.

Physical examination confirms generalized joint hypermobility with increased range of motion beyond normal limits and multiple atrophic scars predominantly on the knees, elbows, and shins. Skin is velvety and hyperextensible with evidence of easy bruising. No cardiovascular abnormalities such as valve prolapse are detected. Musculoskeletal examination reveals mild scoliosis and muscle hypotonia. Neurological and ophthalmologic evaluations are unremarkable.

Laboratory studies including inflammatory markers and coagulation profiles are within normal limits. Radiographs show early degenerative changes consistent with repetitive joint trauma. Genetic testing has not yet been performed.

This clinical picture is consistent with a connective tissue disorder characterized by skin hyperextensibility, abnormal scarring, generalized joint hypermobility with recurrent dislocations, and soft tissue manifestations, pending molecular confirmation.
