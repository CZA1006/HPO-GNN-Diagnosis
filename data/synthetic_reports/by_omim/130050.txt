A 25-year-old female presents with a history of easy bruising and spontaneous arterial ruptures. She reports thin, translucent skin that bruises with minimal trauma, especially visible on the chest and legs. The patient has undergone several hospitalizations for vascular complications, including spontaneous carotid-cavernous fistula and a recent rupture of a medium-sized artery requiring surgical intervention. She also describes episodes of severe abdominal pain; imaging previously revealed spontaneous perforation of the sigmoid colon. There is a history of premature aging of the skin on the hands and feet (acrogeria) and early onset varicose veins. Family history is notable for sudden cardiovascular death in a young relative.

On physical examination, the patient exhibits thin, translucent skin with visible underlying blood vessels. The facial features show prominent large eyes, thin lips, a small chin, and a thin nose. There is evidence of multiple atrophic scars, primarily on bony prominences, and lobeless ears. Peripheral pulses are palpable without murmurs, and joint hypermobility is mild and confined mostly to small joints. No scoliosis or hyperextensible skin is noted.

Laboratory work-up, including coagulation studies, is normal. Vascular imaging reveals multiple aneurysms and areas of arterial dissection. Genetic testing is pending.

This clinical presentation is consistent with a heritable connective tissue disorder characterized by fragile blood vessels, thin translucent skin, vascular and organ rupture risk, and distinctive facial features, pending genetic confirmation.
