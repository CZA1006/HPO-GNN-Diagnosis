An 8-month-old female infant is brought by her parents for evaluation due to unusual facial and limb features noticed since birth. On examination, she has a distinct median cleft of the upper lip and a broad, flat nasal bridge. Eyes are set widely apart (hypertelorism) and there is frontal bossing. Oral cavity inspection reveals a lobulated tongue, with movement mildly restricted, and a duplicated oral frenulum connecting the lip to the gums. She has feeding difficulties but no evidence of airway compromise or cleft palate.

The child's hands and feet show extra digits (postaxial polydactyly) bilaterally, with functional movement of the extra digits. Fingers are otherwise of typical length and shape, with no evidence of fusion or curvature. Foot structure is normal apart from the polydactyly. Neurological examination shows mildly delayed motor milestones and intellectual development appears below expectations for age, with limited social engagement and slow response to stimuli.

Family history reveals a sibling with similar oral features and polydactyly, suggesting a familial pattern. There are no cardiac murmurs, renal enlargement, or evidence of other systemic anomalies on screening. Routine laboratory tests including metabolic panel and renal function are within normal limits. Radiographs confirm well-formed metacarpals and phalanges supporting the extra digits.

The clinical findings reveal a rare congenital disorder characterized by midline upper lip cleft, oral anomalies including a lobulated tongue and accessory frenula, bilateral postaxial polydactyly, hypertelorism, and mild intellectual disability. Further specialist evaluation and genetic testing are planned.
