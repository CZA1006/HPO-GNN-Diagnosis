A 3-year-old male infant is brought for evaluation due to noticeable abnormalities of the head, face, and limbs present at birth. The child exhibits a large head circumference with a prominent, high forehead and widely spaced eyes (hypertelorism). The nasal bridge is broad, and the hairline is high anteriorly. Physical examination reveals the presence of extra digits on both hands and feet, with a combination of preaxial and postaxial polydactyly. Notably, webbing of the skin between some fingers and toes (cutaneous syndactyly) is present, limiting fine motor movements.

Radiographic imaging confirms the fusion of the skin between certain digits without bony fusion in most areas, and extra well-formed phalanges correspond to the supernumerary digits. No abnormalities are noted in the axial skeleton or major organ systems. Developmental milestones are within normal limits, and neurological examination is unremarkable. There are no signs of cognitive impairment, seizures, or hydrocephalus. Family history is negative for similar findings.

The clinical presentation suggests a rare congenital multiple malformation syndrome characterized by abnormalities of craniofacial structure and limbs, specifically macrocephaly, hypertelorism, polydactyly, and cutaneous syndactyly. Follow-up for orthopedic and developmental evaluation is planned, alongside genetic counseling for the family.
