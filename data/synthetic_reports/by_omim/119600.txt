A 6-year-old male presents with notable short stature and abnormal bone development. His parents report that he has unusually mobile shoulders, which can be brought unusually close to the midline due to underdeveloped or absent collarbones. The child has a broad, flat forehead with widely spaced eyes and a depressed nasal bridge. Dental issues are apparent, including delayed loss of baby teeth, presence of extra teeth causing overcrowding, and delayed eruption of adult teeth. The child has a history of recurrent upper respiratory infections and mild hearing problems. There is no cognitive impairment.

On physical examination, the patient exhibits hypoplastic clavicles allowing hypermobility of the shoulders, leading to the ability to approximate his shoulders anteriorly. The chest is cone-shaped with a narrow upper thoracic diameter. The hands show brachydactyly with short distal phalanges and broad thumbs. The face has midface hypoplasia and a pointed jaw contour. Skeletal abnormalities include scoliosis, genu valgum, pes planus, and a wide pubic symphysis. Neurological examination is normal. Dental examination confirms multiple supernumerary teeth and malocclusion.

Radiological imaging shows wide-open cranial sutures and fontanels with presence of wormian bones, hypoplastic clavicles or absent in part, narrow thoracic cage, and various vertebral and pelvic anomalies. Panoramic dental X-rays reveal delayed eruption, supernumerary teeth, and underdeveloped maxillary sinuses.

Laboratory investigations, including metabolic and endocrine tests, are normal. Genetic testing has not been completed yet.
