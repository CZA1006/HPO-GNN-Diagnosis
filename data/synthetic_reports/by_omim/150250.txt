A 3-year-old female presents with multiple joint dislocations noted since birth, primarily involving large joints such as hips, knees, and elbows. The parents report that the child has difficulty walking due to persistent knee dislocations and frequent falls. There is a history of clubfoot with inward and upward turning of both feet, treated with orthotics. The child has short stature with height below the 10th percentile. On examination, she has generalized joint hypermobility but also joint contractures restricting movement in some areas.

Distinctive facial features include a prominent forehead with frontal bossing, wide-set eyes (ocular hypertelorism), a depressed nasal bridge, and flattened midface. The palate shows a midline cleft, and dental examinations reveal malocclusion. The fingers have broad, spatulated tips, especially the thumbs, which appear short and broad. Hearing evaluation suggests mild conductive hearing loss, potentially related to middle ear ossicle anomalies. Spinal examination finds mild scoliosis and significant cervical kyphosis with restricted neck extension, raising concern for potential spinal cord compression.

Radiographic studies confirm multiple dislocations of large joints, supernumerary carpal and tarsal bones, hypoplasia of vertebral bodies, and cervical spine abnormalities with dysraphism of vertebral laminae. Cardiac evaluation is normal, but respiratory assessment notes occasional mild apnea episodes and recurrent respiratory infections. The child’s cognitive development is normal.

This clinical presentation is suggestive of a multisystem skeletal dysplasia with characteristic joint dislocations, craniofacial dysmorphisms, skeletal anomalies, and mild sensory impairment. Genetic testing is pending.
