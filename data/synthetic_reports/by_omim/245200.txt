A 5-month-old male infant is brought for evaluation of progressive irritability, developmental regression, and limb stiffness. Parents report normal early development but note increased startle response and feeding difficulties starting at 3 months. The infant has become less interactive and shows poor head control.

On examination, the infant exhibits hypertonia with exaggerated deep tendon reflexes and clonus. Visual tracking is poor, and there is occasional opisthotonus. Nerve conduction studies reveal demyelinating peripheral neuropathy. MRI of the brain shows symmetric T2 hyperintensities in the periventricular white matter and basal ganglia. Elevated levels of psychosine are detected in plasma.
