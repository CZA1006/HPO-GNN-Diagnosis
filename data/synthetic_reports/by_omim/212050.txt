A 3-year-old female is evaluated for recurrent mucocutaneous candidiasis. Since infancy she has experienced persistent oral thrush, painful fungal diaper rash, and recurrent nail infections. More recently, she required systemic antifungals for esophageal candidiasis. Despite this, her growth has been appropriate, and she has not experienced severe bacterial or viral infections.

On clinical examination, she appears well-nourished with visible white plaques on the oral mucosa and dystrophic fingernails due to chronic fungal infection. Skin examination reveals erythematous, scaly rashes on flexural surfaces. Immunologic evaluation shows normal immunoglobulin levels, normal lymphocyte counts, and preserved vaccine responses. Extensive cultures confirm recurrent Candida albicans growth. No endocrine abnormalities are present at this stage.
