A 12-year-old male presents with a tall and slender physique and a history of progressive skeletal and cardiovascular complaints. He has disproportionately long arms, legs, fingers, and toes, and his arm span exceeds his height. The parents report that he has a curved spine causing back pain and occasional difficulty breathing. The physical examination reveals hypermobile joints, a high-arched palate with crowded teeth, and an abnormal chest shape that either protrudes outward (pectus carinatum) or indents inward (pectus excavatum). His feet are flat and elongated, with hammer toes.

Ophthalmologic examination detects partial dislocation of the lens (ectopia lentis) causing blurred vision and nearsightedness. The patient also has early signs of glaucoma and retinal changes. Cardiovascular assessment reveals a heart murmur due to mitral valve prolapse and aortic regurgitation. Echocardiography confirms dilation of the aortic root with risk for aneurysm formation. The patient reports episodes of palpitations and chest pain radiating to the back.

Neurological and muscular evaluations show no major deficits but musculoskeletal discomfort due to spine deformities like scoliosis and spondylolisthesis. Skin examination identifies stretch marks unrelated to weight changes. Laboratory findings are unremarkable. Imaging studies confirm skeletal abnormalities consistent with abnormal connective tissue.

This presentation is consistent with a multisystem connective tissue disorder involving skeletal elongation, joint hypermobility, ocular lens dislocation, and life-threatening aortic complications. Genetic testing has not yet been performed.
