A 16-year-old male presents with progressive night blindness and peripheral visual field loss. He reports difficulty seeing in dim light since early adolescence. Family history is notable for a cousin with similar visual symptoms and short stature. The patient also reports occasional joint pain but denies significant skeletal deformities.

On examination, funduscopy reveals bone-spicule pigmentation and attenuated retinal vessels. Visual field testing confirms constricted peripheral fields. Skeletal examination shows mild shortening of the fingers without joint contractures. Electroretinography demonstrates markedly reduced rod and cone responses. Radiographs reveal subtle shortening of the metacarpals but no major skeletal malformations.
