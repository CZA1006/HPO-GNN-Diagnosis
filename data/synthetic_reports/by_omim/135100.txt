A 5-year-old male child presents with intermittent episodes of painful soft tissue swellings primarily affecting his neck, shoulders, and back. The parents initially noticed small nodules that appeared after minor trauma or viral illness, which then hardened over several weeks. Over time, these areas became increasingly stiff and restricted his range of motion. The child also has difficulty opening his mouth fully, which is impacting his ability to eat and speak normally. Parents report that he was born with unusually shaped big toes, which appeared short and malformed with inward deviation. Additional findings include stiffness in the spine and progressive limitation in joint mobility.

On physical examination, there are multiple hardened, indurated areas on the posterior neck, upper back, and shoulders, some of which are tender and warm to touch. The limitations in joint movement, particularly of the shoulders and neck, are evident. The great toes show congenital deformities with hallux valgus and shortening. The child is alert and developmentally appropriate for age. No signs of cardiovascular or respiratory distress are present currently, though his breathing appears somewhat restricted with deep inspiration.

Laboratory studies so far are unremarkable. Radiographs reveal heterotopic bone formation in soft tissues around the scapulae and posterior thorax with classic involvement of axial and proximal appendicular skeleton. The bones of the toes show shortened and malformed distal phalanges. No fractures or bone lesions are seen elsewhere.

This clinical course is marked by progressive and episodic heterotopic ossification involving muscles, tendons, and ligaments, triggered by trauma or illness, associated with distinctive congenital skeletal malformations. Genetic testing has yet to be performed.
