A 3-year-old male child presents with a history of a surgically corrected imperforate anus shortly after birth. He was born to healthy, non-consanguineous parents with no notable family history of congenital anomalies. Since infancy, the child has had recurrent ear infections and mild bilateral hearing impairment. Parents have noted differences in the shape of his ears, which appear smaller than normal with overfolded upper helices. Examination of the hands reveals abnormalities of the thumbs, including broadening and, in some areas, the presence of an extra thumb-like structure. The child has also shown mild delays in speech and motor milestones.

On physical examination, the child is well-nourished but shorter than average for age. The ears are dysplastic with characteristic overfolding. The thumbs show preaxial polydactyly and some degree of hypoplasia. Feet appear with overlapping toes and mild deformities. Cardiovascular and abdominal examinations are unremarkable. Renal ultrasound demonstrates displaced kidneys with slight reduction in size. Hearing tests confirm moderate sensorineural hearing loss. Neurological examination is normal.

Laboratory investigations including renal function, metabolic, and endocrine tests are within normal limits. Imaging studies reveal skeletal malformations involving the hands and feet and mild renal anomalies. No genetic testing has been conducted so far. The clinical features suggest a multisystem congenital syndrome involving anorectal malformations, ear dysplasia, limb abnormalities, hearing loss, and renal involvement, pending genetic confirmation.
