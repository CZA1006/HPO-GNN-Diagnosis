A newborn male presents with characteristic craniofacial abnormalities noted at birth. The parents report feeding difficulties and noisy breathing. Facial features include downward-slanting eyes (palpebral fissures), absent or notched lower eyelids (colobomas), sparse or absent eyelashes on the lower lids, and underdeveloped or absent cheekbones (malar hypoplasia), resulting in a sunken appearance of the cheeks. The mandible is small and underdeveloped (micrognathia), with a retruded chin. His ears are small, malformed, and low-set with narrow or absent external ear canals.

The infant has a high-arched or cleft palate contributing to feeding difficulties. Hearing tests reveal conductive hearing loss likely due to malformed middle ear ossicles. Breathing difficulties are notable, with occasional apneic episodes and upper airway obstruction from narrow respiratory passages. The oral cavity exhibits dental malocclusion and limited mouth opening. Neurological exam is normal, and developmental milestones are typical for age.

Radiographic imaging confirms hypoplasia of the zygomatic bones, mandible, and middle ear structures. The rest of the skeletal survey is unremarkable. Cardiovascular and abdominal examinations are normal. This presentation indicates a congenital craniofacial disorder characterized by bilateral and symmetrical facial bone hypoplasia, ear malformations, hearing loss, and respiratory and feeding difficulties. Genetic testing has not yet been conducted.
