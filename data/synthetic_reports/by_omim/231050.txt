A 5-year-old female is referred for disproportionate short stature, joint contractures, and characteristic facial features. Parents note that she has a “happy” facial expression and developed restricted joint mobility early in childhood. Mild respiratory difficulties and recurrent upper airway infections have occurred.

On physical examination, the child is small for age with short limbs and a small chest. Facial features include a broad forehead, upturned nose, and prominent cheeks. Joint exam reveals limited range of motion with contractures at elbows and knees. Cardiac auscultation detects a systolic murmur; echocardiography reveals mitral valve thickening. Radiographs show shortened long bones with mild epiphyseal dysplasia.
