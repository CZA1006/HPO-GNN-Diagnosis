A 30-year-old female presents with gradually declining kidney function identified during evaluation for persistent mild fatigue and elevated serum creatinine. She reports increased urination, including nocturia, and occasional joint pain with swelling, particularly affecting the big toe, consistent with episodes of gout. There is no significant proteinuria or hematuria on urinalysis. The patient denies hypertension, edema, or symptoms suggestive of urinary tract infection.

Family history is significant for multiple relatives with chronic kidney disease progressing to dialysis in their 50s, suggestive of autosomal dominant inheritance. Physical examination is unremarkable, with no peripheral edema, normal blood pressure, and no joint deformities. Laboratory investigations show elevated serum creatinine (estimated GFR consistent with stage 3 CKD), hyperuricemia, and normal electrolytes. Urinalysis reveals bland sediment, minimal proteinuria, and no casts.

Renal ultrasound demonstrates kidneys of normal size without cysts or obstruction. A kidney biopsy from an affected family member reviewed shows interstitial fibrosis, tubular atrophy with thickening and lamellation of tubular basement membranes, and no significant glomerular abnormalities. No immune complex deposits or inflammation were noted.

This clinical picture suggests a hereditary tubulointerstitial kidney disease with autosomal dominant inheritance pattern, characterized by slow progression to end-stage kidney disease, bland urinary sediment, and hyperuricemia, often complicated by gout. Genetic testing is planned for further diagnostic clarification.
