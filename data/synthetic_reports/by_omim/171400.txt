A 36-year-old male presents for evaluation after developing episodic hypertension and palpitations over the last year, sometimes associated with headaches, sweating, and anxiety. He has experienced intermittent diarrhea for several months and has lost approximately 5 kilograms without intentional dietary restriction. Past medical history is notable for surgical excision of a thyroid mass in his late twenties, which was diagnosed as a medullary carcinoma. Family history includes a mother who required thyroid surgery due to malignancy and an uncle who was treated for an adrenal tumor in his forties.

Physical examination reveals elevated blood pressure and tachycardia during symptomatic episodes. The neck scar from prior thyroid surgery is well healed, and there is no palpable cervical lymphadenopathy or thyroid enlargement. Palpation of the abdomen is normal, but the patient describes pain during episodes of flushing and palpitations. Mild muscle wasting is noted but no evidence of parathyroid masses or bony tenderness.

Laboratory investigations show persistently elevated plasma metanephrines and urinary catecholamines. Further workup with an abdominal CT identifies bilateral adrenal masses highly suggestive of catecholamine-secreting tumors. Serum calcitonin remains increased post-surgery. Parathyroid hormone and serum calcium levels are at the limits of normal. Screening for other endocrine abnormalities such as hyperparathyroidism is pending.

The clinical pattern strongly suggests a multisystem endocrine disorder marked by episodic catecholamine excess, a personal and family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, as well as biochemical evidence of adrenal and possible parathyroid involvement. Plans for further evaluation and multidisciplinary management are underway.
