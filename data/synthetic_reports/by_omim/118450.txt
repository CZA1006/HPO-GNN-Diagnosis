A 10-month-old female infant presents with persistent jaundice and failure to thrive since early infancy. She was born to healthy, unrelated parents after an unremarkable pregnancy. The parents report yellowing of the skin and sclera, pale stools, and dark urine. The infant has had several episodes of intense itching. On physical examination, the child exhibits characteristic facial features including a broad forehead, deep-set and widely spaced eyes, a prominent nasal bridge, and a pointed chin. Cardiac examination reveals a systolic murmur, and further echocardiogram identifies pulmonary artery stenosis. Spine radiographs demonstrate characteristic butterfly vertebrae. Ophthalmological evaluation shows posterior embryotoxon and mild strabismus. There is no evidence of hepatosplenomegaly, but liver function tests indicate cholestasis.

Additional laboratory studies reveal elevated bilirubin and liver enzymes, along with vitamin deficiencies consistent with fat malabsorption. Renal ultrasound is normal. Growth parameters indicate delayed weight gain and mild short stature. Developmental milestones are slightly delayed. Genetic testing has not yet been performed.

This combination of progressive cholestasis, congenital heart defects, distinctive facial features, vertebral anomalies, and ocular findings suggests a multisystem autosomal dominant disorder affecting liver, heart, skeletal, and eye development, pending genetic confirmation
