A 31-year-old male presents with newly diagnosed diabetes mellitus at age 29, initially treated with oral hypoglycemic agents but now requiring insulin therapy due to worsening glycemic control. The patient reports a history of recurrent kidney infections and imaging studies revealed multiple renal cysts and unilateral renal hypoplasia. He has experienced intermittent episodes of gout and hyperuricemia. There is a history of undescended testes and infertility, with genital tract malformations noted on urological examination. Liver function tests show mild but persistent elevations without clinical liver disease. Neurodevelopmental assessment reveals mild cognitive impairment and features consistent with an autism spectrum disorder. Family history is positive for diabetes and kidney disease in first-degree relatives.

On physical examination, the patient has normal vital signs with no significant findings aside from mild obesity. Abdominal examination reveals no palpable masses or tenderness. Laboratory studies confirm hyperglycemia, elevated serum uric acid, and mild hypomagnesemia. Kidney function tests are within normal limits but urinalysis shows low-level proteinuria. Imaging studies confirm renal cystic changes with renal agenesis on one side and a dysplastic contralateral kidney. Pancreatic imaging suggests hypoplasia with reduced exocrine function.

The constellation of early-onset diabetes, cystic renal disease, genital tract anomalies, hyperuricemia with gout, and neurodevelopmental abnormalities suggests a multisystem disorder involving renal, endocrine, reproductive, hepatic, and neurological systems. Genetic testing for specific gene alterations has not been performed yet.
