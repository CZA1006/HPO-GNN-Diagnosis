A 7-year-old female presents with several flat, light brown patches on her skin, primarily distributed over the trunk and extremities. These patches, identified as café au lait spots, have been present since early childhood and number more than six. The child also exhibits freckling in the armpits and groin area, which became noticeable by age 5. Physical exam reveals multiple small, soft bumps under the skin consistent with neurofibromas, some located on the face and trunk. Eye examination identifies small, yellowish-brown nodules on the iris (Lisch nodules), which do not affect vision.

The child has mild scoliosis and reports occasional headaches but no history of seizures. A recent vision screening showed mild strabismus. Developmental assessment reveals mild learning difficulties with attention deficit and speech delay. There is no significant family history, though a parent has similar skin patches. Neurological exam is otherwise normal without focal deficits.

MRI brain reveals a small optic pathway glioma, consistent with a benign tumor affecting the optic nerve. No other CNS tumors are detected. No skeletal abnormalities such as bowing of limbs or pseudoarthrosis are observed at this time. Cardiovascular examination is normal with no hypertension. Hearing assessment is unremarkable.

This clinical presentation suggests a genetic disorder with multiple neurocutaneous manifestations, benign tumors of nerve tissue, skin pigmentation changes, CNS involvement with low-grade tumors, and mild neurodevelopmental delays. Further diagnostic evaluation including genetic testing is planned for confirmation.
