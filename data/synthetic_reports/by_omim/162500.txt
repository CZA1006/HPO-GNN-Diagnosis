A 16-year-old male presents with multiple episodes of numbness, tingling, and weakness affecting different parts of his arms and legs over the past few years. The symptoms often follow minor trauma or prolonged pressure on certain areas such as the elbows, wrists, and knees. Episodes have included sudden foot drop, weakness of the hand grip, and difficulty in finger movements, all corresponding to nerves vulnerable to compression. He reports that symptoms typically develop acutely, last from days to several weeks, and then partially or completely resolve. There is occasional mild pain but predominantly sensory symptoms.

Physical examination during symptom-free intervals reveals mild weakness in the peroneal and ulnar nerve distributions, slight muscle atrophy, and decreased sensation to light touch and pinprick in affected areas. Reflexes are reduced in the affected limbs. There is no evidence of upper motor neuron involvement. Gait assessment shows mild foot drop during certain episodes. No cranial nerve involvement or autonomic symptoms are reported.

Nerve conduction studies demonstrate focal slowing and conduction block at common compression sites such as the fibular head, ulnar groove, and carpal tunnel, supporting demyelinating neuropathy. Electromyography shows signs of chronic and acute denervation in muscles supplied by affected nerves. Family history is significant for similarly affected relatives with recurrent episodes of numbness and weakness.

No systemic illness or metabolic abnormalities have been identified in blood tests. MRI scans of the spine and brain are normal. The clinical presentation is suggestive of a hereditary neuropathy characterized by susceptibility to pressure-induced focal nerve palsies with recurrent episodes and partial recovery. Further genetic testing will be essential for confirming the diagnosis and guiding management.
