A 10-year-old male presents with gradually progressive joint stiffness that has limited his ability to raise his arms and clenched his fists over the past few years. The parents note that the child's skin appears thicker and less pliable than usual, especially on the arms and legs. He has a history of recurrent respiratory infections and noisy breathing, sometimes accompanied by a dry cough and stridor. Facial features are distinctive with a prominent forehead, flat midface, narrow mouth with thin lips, and a relatively small jaw. Hearing loss has been noted, and developmental delays with mild cognitive impairment are apparent. The child has a muscular appearance with apparent muscle firmness despite limited range of motion. Growth is below average, with short stature and early-onset obesity.

On examination, joint mobility is markedly reduced with contractures particularly affecting the elbows, shoulders, and fingers. The skin is thickened with a velvety texture. Respiratory examination reveals mild wheezing and loud breath sounds. Cardiovascular examination is notable for a mild systolic murmur. Neurologic examination confirms mild delays in cognitive processing and attention deficits. Audiological testing shows sensorineural hearing loss. Ophthalmological exam indicates refractive errors without major structural abnormalities.

Radiological imaging demonstrates thickened cortical bone and scoliosis. Pulmonary function tests reveal restrictive lung disease likely related to chest wall stiffness and airway narrowing. Echocardiography shows mild structural heart abnormalities including narrowing of the aorta and thickened pericardium. Laboratory tests are generally unremarkable. Genetic testing for confirmation has not yet been performed.

Overall, the clinical features encompass a progressive connective tissue disorder involving skin thickening, joint contractures, respiratory complications, cardiac anomalies, distinct craniofacial features, developmental delay, and hearing impairment.
