A 27-year-old female presents with a history of multiple recurrent skin lesions primarily on the face, ears, and sun-exposed areas of the limbs. These lesions began as reddish macules which progressed to papules, then nodules with central crater-like ulcers. Each lesion persists for several months before spontaneously resolving, leaving pitted scars with irregular, overhanging edges. The affected skin often shows localized areas of scarring that are cosmetically disfiguring. The patient notes that new lesions periodically develop, sometimes numbering more than a hundred over several years.

Family history reveals similar skin lesions in multiple relatives, indicating a strong familial pattern. The age of onset among family members varies widely, from childhood to the elderly. The patient denies systemic symptoms such as weight loss, fever, or other organ involvement.

Physical examination identifies numerous depressed scars on the sun-exposed regions, and a few active reddish nodular lesions with central ulceration. There is no lymphadenopathy or signs of systemic illness. The patient’s general physical and neurological exams are normal.

Histopathological examination of a biopsy obtained from an active lesion shows well-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma features with deep dermal invasion, accompanied by lymphocytic infiltration and central keratinization forming thick horny plugs. Imaging studies have not revealed any distant metastases. Treatment to date has involved local excision of some lesions, but spontaneous regression occurred in others.

Laboratory investigations, including routine blood work and systemic tumor markers, are within normal limits. Genetic testing has not yet been conducted. The clinical presentation suggests a rare familial skin disorder characterized by multiple locally invasive tumors with a natural history of self-healing and scar formation.
