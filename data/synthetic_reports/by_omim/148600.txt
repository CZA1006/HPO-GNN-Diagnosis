A 15-year-old adolescent presents with gradually developing numerous small, hard bumps on the palms of his hands and soles of his feet. The parents report that the lesions started appearing in early adolescence and have increased in number over time. The bumps are rough, keratotic papules often with central indentations and are irregularly distributed on the pressure points of the palms and soles. The patient occasionally experiences pain and tenderness in these areas, especially after prolonged walking or standing. There is no significant scaling or redness around the lesions, and no involvement of other skin areas is noted.

On physical examination, multiple tiny hyperkeratotic papules with central depressions are observed over the palms and soles, with some areas showing coalescence into larger plaques, particularly over the heel and base of fingers. The surrounding skin appears normal without inflammation or fissures. No nail abnormalities or other dermatologic findings are present. There is variability in lesion severity among family members, but no systemic symptoms are reported.

Histopathological analysis of biopsied lesions shows pronounced hyperkeratosis with irregular epidermal thickening. Routine laboratory tests are unremarkable. The clinical features are consistent with a rare hereditary condition involving punctate keratoderma localized to the palms and soles with onset in adolescence, marked by multiple hyperkeratotic papules causing discomfort on pressure areas. Genetic testing has not been performed yet.
