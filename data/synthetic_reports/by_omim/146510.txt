A 3-year-old female is brought in for evaluation due to congenital abnormalities and developmental delay. Her family reports she was born with extra fingers and toes, with webbing noted between some digits. At birth, she had a cleft uvula and difficulties with feeding. Parents also observed noisy breathing and recurrent episodes of choking. Facial examination reveals a broad forehead, a flat and wide nasal bridge, and small, low-set ears angled backwards. The child has a small mouth and tongue and demonstrates some degree of hypotonia and delayed motor milestones.

Neurological assessment identifies mild cognitive delay with poor coordination. MRI of the brain reveals a large non-malignant mass in the hypothalamic area consistent with a hamartoma. Respiratory evaluation shows the presence of a bifid epiglottis contributing to airway obstruction. Gastrointestinal examination identifies imperforate anus that was surgically corrected shortly after birth. Genitourinary evaluation shows anomalies including an underdeveloped scrotum and hypospadias. Cardiac and renal ultrasounds reveal no significant abnormalities.

The infant’s overall presentation involves multiple congenital anomalies affecting the digits, airway, brain structure, and genitourinary tract, along with developmental and feeding difficulties. This pattern suggests a complex multisystem developmental disorder requiring multidisciplinary care. Genetic testing has not yet been conducted.
