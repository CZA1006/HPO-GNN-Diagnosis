Acromesomelic dysplasia 1, Maroteaux type (OMIM:602875)

The patient is an 8-year-old boy presenting with disproportionate short stature. Pregnancy and delivery were unremarkable. His growth plateaued around age 2, and his parents noticed progressively shortened forearms and lower legs. Family history reveals no known skeletal conditions. On examination, the child displays severe short stature (<3rd percentile) with disproportionately short forearms, hands, lower legs, and feet. Facial appearance includes mild frontal bossing and depressed nasal bridge. Clinical evaluation shows normal intelligence and absence of visceral organ involvement. Radiographic skeletal survey demonstrates rhizomelic and mesomelic shortening, metaphyseal irregularity, and cone-shaped epiphyses in the hands. Laboratory studies including calcium, phosphate, vitamin D, and parathyroid hormone were within normal limits.
