The patient is a 17-year-old female presenting with progressive difficulties seeing in dim light since early adolescence. She notes gradual constriction of her peripheral visual fields and now struggles with night vision. Family history reveals that her maternal grandmother experienced similar vision loss in her teens. On examination, visual acuity is mildly reduced, and visual field testing demonstrates concentric peripheral field restriction. Fundus examination shows attenuated retinal vessels and bone-spicule pigmentary changes in the mid-periphery. Optical coherence tomography demonstrates thinning of the outer retinal layers, and electroretinography shows markedly reduced scotopic response, consistent with diminished rod function.

On physical examination, the patient exhibits sparse scalp hair, eyebrows, and eyelashes, with otherwise normal skin and nails. Ophthalmic exam shows bilateral decreased central vision, fundus examination with pigmentary changes at the macula, and OCT showing early degeneration of the photoreceptor layer in the central retina. Visual field testing reveals central scotomas. Routine blood work was normal, and no systemic anomalies detected.

Retinitis pigmentosa 19 (OMIM:601718)

The patient is a 26-year-old male with progressive vision problems since early adolescence. He reports worsening night vision and difficulty recognizing faces in low-light situations. Central vision is still relatively preserved but peripheral visual fields are markedly reduced. Family history is significant for visual impairment in his mother, who lost functional vision in her 40s. On ophthalmologic examination, funduscopy reveals narrowed retinal vessels and pigment deposits in the mid-peripheral retina. Optical coherence tomography shows thinning of photoreceptor layers, with central foveal preservation. Electroretinography demonstrates severely reduced rod and cone responses. Other systemic examinations and laboratory studies are unremarkable.
