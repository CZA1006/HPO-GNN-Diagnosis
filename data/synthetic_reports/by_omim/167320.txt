A 48-year-old male presents with progressive muscle weakness that began in his fourth decade, initially noticed as difficulty climbing stairs and raising his arms above his shoulders. Over time, he developed weakness affecting both proximal and distal muscles, leading to trouble with fine motor tasks and frequent falls. The patient also reports persistent bone pain, especially in the hips and lower back, which has worsened over several years. Physical examination reveals marked muscle atrophy predominantly in the shoulder and hip girdle muscles, with weakness graded 3/5 in these areas and mild distal involvement. There is notable difficulty rising from a seated position and climbing stairs.

Skeletal exam demonstrates bony enlargement and deformities of the pelvis and spine. Radiographs confirm changes consistent with abnormal bone remodeling involving the long bones, pelvis, and vertebrae, without evidence of fractures. Cognitive evaluation identifies early signs of frontotemporal dysfunction, including difficulties with word finding, calculation, and comprehension, accompanied by mild behavioral changes such as apathy and socially inappropriate remarks. Brain MRI shows focal atrophy in the frontal and temporal lobes with ventricular enlargement.

Laboratory studies reveal normal creatine kinase levels and elevated alkaline phosphatase consistent with increased bone turnover. Electromyography shows myopathic changes with fibrillation potentials. Muscle biopsy demonstrates characteristic rimmed vacuoles and inclusions. Pulmonary and cardiac assessments are unremarkable.

The clinical picture suggests a rare, progressive multisystem disorder involving adult-onset inclusion body myopathy, early-onset bone disease with abnormal remodeling, and frontotemporal dementia with characteristic neuroimaging findings. Genetic testing is planned for confirmation and family counseling.
