A 12-year-old male is evaluated for muscle cramps, tetany, and recurrent seizures. Symptoms began in late childhood and are associated with episodes of dizziness and fatigue. His parents note that symptoms worsen during periods of fasting or vigorous exercise. Family history reveals an affected sibling with similar symptoms.

On examination, the patient appears alert with mild tremors and a positive Chvostek sign. Blood pressure is normal. Laboratory studies show persistent hypomagnesemia with secondary hypocalcemia. Urinary magnesium excretion is elevated, indicating renal magnesium wasting. Serum potassium is mildly decreased. Electrocardiogram demonstrates a prolonged QT interval.
