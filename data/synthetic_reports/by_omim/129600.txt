A 12-year-old male presents with progressive visual disturbances characterized by blurred and fluctuating vision. His parents noticed difficulty in focusing and frequent complaints of double vision since early childhood. There is a history of nearsightedness (myopia) and astigmatism diagnosed during routine eye exams. The patient has no history of trauma or other systemic illnesses. Family history reveals several relatives with similar eye problems, but no known cardiovascular or skeletal abnormalities.

On ophthalmologic examination, the patient exhibits bilateral lens dislocation (ectopia lentis) predominantly in the superotemporal direction, confirmed by slit-lamp evaluation after pupil dilation. The lenses appear subluxated but remain partially within the pupillary axis. Visual acuity is decreased, and refractive errors are pronounced. The irides appear normal without transillumination defects. Intraocular pressure is within normal limits, and fundoscopic exam shows no retinal detachment or vitreous abnormalities. Skeletal examination reveals no features of Marfan syndrome or other connective tissue disorders. Cardiovascular assessment including echocardiography is normal.

Laboratory investigations, including plasma homocysteine levels, are normal, excluding homocystinuria. Genetic testing is pending.
