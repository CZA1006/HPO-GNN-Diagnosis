A 12-year-old male presents for evaluation after recurrent episodes of abdominal pain and rectal bleeding over the past year. His mother reports that since infancy, he has had numerous small, dark brown and bluish freckle-like spots distributed around his lips, oral mucosa, fingertips, soles, and perianal region. These spots became apparent before age five and were sometimes mistaken for normal freckles; they have persisted into adolescence with some fading on the skin but remaining prominent inside the mouth.

Family history reveals that the patient's maternal aunt and grandmother required surgical interventions for intestinal polyps in their 30s and 40s, and an uncle was diagnosed with colon cancer in his fifties. On examination, the patient is healthy-appearing with normal growth, but multiple characteristic mucocutaneous pigmented macules are seen around the lips and oral cavity. His skin otherwise shows no abnormal findings.

Abdominal and rectal examination reveal tenderness but no palpable masses. Laboratory assessment demonstrates iron-deficiency anemia, with low hemoglobin and hematocrit. Upper and lower gastrointestinal endoscopy identify multiple benign hamartomatous polyps in the jejunum, stomach, and colon, some of which show evidence of chronic bleeding but no malignancy. There are no signs of acute obstruction, intussusception, or extraintestinal masses on imaging. Breast and testicular examination are normal. Routine cancer screenings are planned.

The patient’s clinical course features recurrent GI symptoms due to polyps, longstanding mucocutaneous pigmentation, a positive family history of polyposis and cancer, and anemia consistent with ongoing bleeding. Surveillance and multidisciplinary management are being established to monitor for polyp-related complications and associated malignancies.
