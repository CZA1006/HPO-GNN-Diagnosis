A 7-year-old female presents with a history of short stature and multiple skeletal abnormalities. She is the first child of healthy, non-related parents with no known family history of bone disorders or genetic conditions. The child was born at term after an uncomplicated pregnancy and delivery. Since infancy, the parents noticed a distinct facial appearance described as coarse features with prominent eyebrows that almost meet in the middle, widely spaced eyes with downward slanting eyelids, a flattened broad nose bridge, and a small lower jaw. The child has experienced frequent bone fractures over the past few years, especially involving the fingers and toes, which have progressively shortened. She complains of joint hypermobility but also stiffness in some areas. The parents noted delayed closure of the soft spots (fontanels) on the skull and persistent open sutures. The child has recurrent dental problems, including early loss of baby teeth and mobility of others. Growth has been consistently below the expected percentile.

On physical examination, she exhibits short stature, coarse facial features with synophrys and midface flattening, and micrognathia. There are noticeable deformities of the hands and feet, with short, broad fingers and toes showing signs of bone resorption particularly at the distal phalanges. The spine is mildly curved with scoliosis. There is evidence of joint hypermobility alongside restricted movement in some joints. The fontanels remain open beyond infancy. No cardiac murmurs or abdominal abnormalities are detected. Neurological examination is normal.

Laboratory investigations including metabolic and endocrine panels are within normal range. Radiographic imaging reveals osteoporosis, acro-osteolysis (bone loss at the distal finger and toe tips), widened cranial sutures with wormian bones, scoliosis, and kyphosis. There is evidence of basilar invagination and flattening of the skull base. Renal ultrasound shows no cystic changes. Genetic testing has not been performed yet.
