Cutis laxa, autosomal recessive, type IIB (OMIM:612940)

The patient is a 3-year-old female born to consanguineous parents, presenting with generalized loose, inelastic skin and developmental delay. The pregnancy was complicated by intrauterine growth restriction and polyhydramnios. At birth, she exhibited redundant skin folds prominently over the face, neck, and extremities. Family history is negative for connective tissue disorders. Physical examination reveals coarse facies with redundant, pendulous skin, poor wound healing, hypotonia, and mild hepatomegaly. Cardiovascular examination identifies mild mitral valve prolapse and dilated aortic root. Radiological studies reveal emphysema-like changes in the lungs and mild skeletal abnormalities. Laboratory investigations, including metabolic screening and routine blood tests, are unremarkable. Skin biopsy shows fragmented elastic fibers on histological analysis. Management involves multidisciplinary care addressing pulmonary, cardiac, and dermatologic complications.
