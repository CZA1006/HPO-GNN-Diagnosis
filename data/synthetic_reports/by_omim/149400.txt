A newborn male infant presents with excessive muscle stiffness and an exaggerated startle response to sudden sounds, touches, or movements, noted from birth. The infant exhibits generalized hypertonia, particularly involving the trunk, with episodes of muscle rigidity lasting seconds to minutes after being startled. After these episodes, the infant temporarily becomes very stiff and may exhibit apnea or difficulty breathing. Parents report that the baby often moves slowly and has difficulty initiating voluntary movements. The exaggerated startle reflex has led to frequent episodes of falling or sudden jerks when the infant is handled or exposed to loud noises.

Physical examination reveals generalized muscle stiffness with brisk reflexes and occasional myoclonic jerks, especially when falling asleep. No seizures have been documented, but intermittent apnea episodes raise concern for respiratory compromise. The infant has no structural abnormalities or dysmorphic features. Feeding difficulties are noted due to rigidity affecting oral motor coordination. There is a history of congenital dislocation of the hips and inguinal hernias.

Laboratory and imaging studies including brain MRI and EEG are unremarkable. Routine blood tests and metabolic panels show no abnormalities. The clinical picture is consistent with a neurological disorder characterized by neonatal hypertonia, exaggerated startle reflex, and potential apnea events. Genetic testing remains to be performed.
