A 15-year-old male is referred for evaluation of chronic anemia and intermittent episodes of dark urine. His parents report that he has experienced lifelong fatigue, poor exercise tolerance, and occasional jaundice during febrile illnesses. Several times, particularly after exposure to cold environments, he has developed hemoglobinuria. Parents also note that he often complains of abdominal pain, and an ultrasound during a recent hospital admission showed splenomegaly.

Pregnancy and birth history were normal, and he was born at term without complications. Neonatal jaundice required phototherapy in the first week of life. Developmental milestones were achieved normally. His past medical history is remarkable for recurrent anemia requiring occasional transfusions, especially after infections. There is no history of autoimmune or connective tissue disorders. Family history reveals that his mother suffered from mild anemia in childhood of unclear cause, but no formal diagnosis was established.

On physical examination, the patient is alert but appears pale. There is mild scleral icterus. The spleen is palpable 4 cm below the left costal margin, while the liver is not enlarged. Cardiovascular and respiratory examinations are within normal limits. No lymphadenopathy or bone deformities are noted. Skin examination shows no rash, ulcerations, or livedo. Extremities reveal no acrocyanosis or Raynaud-like changes, but the parents emphasize that his hemoglobinuria typically occurs after cold exposure.

Laboratory studies reveal chronic hemolytic anemia with hemoglobin at 9.2 g/dL, reticulocyte count elevated at 8%, and indirect bilirubin mildly increased. Haptoglobin is decreased, and lactate dehydrogenase is elevated. Peripheral blood smear shows anisopoikilocytosis and increased numbers of stomatocytes. Serum electrolytes and renal function are within normal limits, though urinalysis demonstrates intermittent hemoglobinuria without proteinuria. Direct antiglobulin (Coombs) test is negative. Osmotic fragility testing shows increased red blood cell lysis, especially on cooling. Ektacytometry reveals abnormal red blood cell deformability with temperature sensitivity. Bone marrow aspirate is hypercellular with erythroid predominance.

Additional tests include abdominal ultrasound confirming splenomegaly and normal liver echotexture. Cardiac echocardiography shows no abnormalities. The patient has not yet undergone genetic testing, but analysis is being planned to clarify the molecular basis of his hemolytic disorder.
