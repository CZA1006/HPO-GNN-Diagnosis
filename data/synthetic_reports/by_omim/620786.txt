A 16-year-old male presented with progressive movement abnormalities characterized by bradykinesia, tremor, and dystonia starting in late childhood. He also reports mild cognitive difficulties and episodes of behavioral changes, including irritability and apathy. There is no significant family history reported. Neurological examination demonstrated rigidity, hypomimia, and dysarthria with no evidence of pyramidal signs. Brain computed tomography identified bilateral symmetrical calcifications predominantly localized in the basal ganglia and thalami. MRI showed no significant cortical atrophy or other structural abnormalities. Routine blood tests, including calcium and phosphate metabolism panels, were within normal limits. Metabolic and infectious causes of basal ganglia calcification were excluded. Management has focused on symptomatic treatments including dopaminergic agents and supportive therapies.
Aplasia cutis-enamel dysplasia syndrome (OMIM:620789)

The patient is a 4-year-old female with well-demarcated congenital absence of skin localized to the scalp and multiple enamel defects affecting both primary and permanent dentition. She was born at term to non-consanguineous parents with an uneventful pregnancy. Physical examination revealed a 5 cm alopecic scalp scar resembling aplasia cutis congenita. Dental examination showed hypoplastic enamel with brown discoloration, increased caries risk, and structural weakness. No other congenital anomalies were identified on systemic examination. Radiographic imaging confirmed enamel hypoplasia with normal tooth morphology. The patient is under multidisciplinary care including dermatologic management for the scalp defect and preventive dental care.
Neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia and seizures (OMIM:620790)

A 9-month-old female infant was evaluated for delayed developmental milestones and recurrent seizures beginning at 3 months of age. Birth history was normal, and there is no known family history of neurological disorders. The patient exhibited global hypotonia and poor head control. Clinical seizures included tonic and clonic movements affecting limbs, often clustering in series. EEG revealed multifocal epileptiform discharges with diffuse slowing. Brain MRI demonstrated cortical dysplasia with delayed myelination and mild cerebral atrophy. Metabolic screens were negative. The patient is managed with multiple antiepileptic drugs, physical therapy, and developmental support services.
Muscular dystrophy, limb-girdle, autosomal recessive 29 (OMIM:620793)

The patient is an 11-year-old male with insidious onset of proximal muscle weakness, difficulty climbing stairs, and frequent falls starting around age 8. He reports mild muscle cramps but denies sensory symptoms. Examination reveals waddling gait, scapular winging, and symmetric weakness of shoulder and hip girdle muscles. Serum creatine kinase is elevated at 8 times the upper limit of normal. Electromyography shows a myopathic pattern. Muscle biopsy demonstrates dystrophic changes with variation in fiber size, increased connective tissue, and mild inflammation. Cardiac and respiratory assessments are unremarkable. The patient is enrolled in physical rehabilitation and is being monitored for potential progression.
Deafness, autosomal recessive 124 (OMIM:620794)

The patient is a 6-year-old male child with congenital bilateral sensorineural hearing loss identified through newborn screening. There is no significant family history or associated vestibular symptoms. Otoscopic examination is normal. Audiological testing including auditory brainstem response and otoacoustic emissions confirms moderate to severe sensorineural hearing loss bilaterally. Imaging of the temporal bones via CT scan shows normal cochlear and vestibular anatomy. There are no additional neurological or systemic findings. Early amplification with hearing aids and enrollment in speech therapy programs has been initiated.
Autoinflammation with episodic fever and immune dysregulation (OMIM:620795)

A 7-year-old female presents with recurrent febrile episodes lasting several days, accompanied by fatigue, arthralgias, and skin rash manifesting as erythematous plaques. Symptoms started in early childhood without obvious triggers. Physical examination during episodes shows lymphadenopathy and hepatosplenomegaly. Laboratory evaluation reveals significant elevation of inflammatory markers including CRP and ESR, transient cytopenias, and hyperferritinemia. Immune profiling identifies impaired regulatory T-cell function and abnormal cytokine release. Bone marrow biopsy excludes malignancy. The patient is undergoing immunomodulatory therapy including corticosteroids and biologic agents, with ongoing clinical monitoring.
Proteasome-associated autoinflammatory syndrome 6 (OMIM:620796)

The patient is a 2-year-old male with early-onset recurrent fever, diffuse skin rash, and arthritis. Episodes typically last 3–7 days with spontaneous resolution. The child exhibits failure to thrive and developmental delay. Laboratory studies during flares demonstrate neutrophilic leukocytosis, elevated acute phase reactants, and elevated liver enzymes. Genetic testing for common autoinflammatory syndromes is negative. Skin biopsy shows neutrophilic dermatosis. Immunological evaluation reveals impaired proteasome function. Management involves anti-inflammatory medications including corticosteroids and biologic agents targeting cytokines.
Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy 116 (OMIM:620806)

The patient is a 5-month-old female admitted for refractory seizures beginning within weeks of birth. Seizure semiology includes tonic, clonic, and epileptic spasms, with poor response to multiple antiepileptic drugs. Development is markedly delayed; there is absence of social smile and minimal spontaneous movements. Clinical examination reveals generalized hypotonia and absent gag reflex. EEG shows hypsarrhythmia and multifocal epileptiform abnormalities. Brain MRI demonstrates diffuse cortical atrophy and delayed myelination without focal lesions. Metabolic screening and infectious causes have been excluded. Multidisciplinary management is ongoing with emphasis on seizure control and supportive care.
Immunodeficiency 121 with autoinflammation (OMIM:620807)

The patient is a 14-year-old male with recurrent infections, chronic inflammation, and intermittent fevers since infancy. He presents with persistent lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, and hepatomegaly. Laboratory investigations reveal neutropenia, anemia, elevated inflammatory markers, and hypergammaglobulinemia. Immunophenotyping reveals reduced numbers of regulatory T cells and increased proinflammatory cytokine production. Bone marrow biopsy is reactive with no malignant changes. Treatment includes immunosuppressive therapy and supportive infection prophylaxis. Regular follow-up is essential for monitoring disease progression.
MHC class I deficiency 2 (OMIM:620813)

An 8-year-old female presents with recurrent respiratory and gastrointestinal infections from infancy. She exhibits failure to thrive and persistent lymphadenopathy. Laboratory workup demonstrates selective absence of HLA class I molecules on peripheral blood lymphocytes and reduced CD8+ T-cell counts. Immunoglobulin levels are normal. Flow cytometry excludes other lymphocyte subsets abnormalities. Chest CT shows bronchiectasis. The patient is managed with immunoglobulin replacement therapy, antibiotic prophylaxis, and respiratory physiotherapy.
